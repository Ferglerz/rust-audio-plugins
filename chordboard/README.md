# Chordboard

A MIDI-only harmony and strumming effect with Pleasant UI. VST3 and CLAP; no internal sound engine. Place Chordboard before an instrument and enable MIDI output/routing in the host. Stereo audio passes through unchanged.

## Play

Click the chord keyboard to focus the editor. QWERTY captures playing keys only while focused. The number row (`1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 0 - +`) selects major chords, `Q W E R T Y U I O P [ ]` selects minor chords, and `A S D F G H J K L ; ' Enter` selects dominant seventh chords. The `+` position uses the physical `=/+` key without requiring Shift; Enter is labelled `ENT`. In **Chromatic** and **Fifths**, each staggered row includes all twelve roots; the layout changes their order. **Degrees** puts diatonic scale triads on Q, one brighter chord quality on the number row, and one darker quality on A: diminished → minor → major → augmented, clamped at the ends. Pentatonic scales use triads from their parent major or natural minor scale. **Passing** keeps Q and A, but places a chromatic approach chord immediately below each Q-row degree on the number key just before it, inheriting the preceding degree’s quality. In C major, 2=C♯, 3=D♯, 5=F♯, 6=G♯, and 7=A♯; key 1 and natural semitone gaps remain inactive. Wider scale gaps use the approach note below the next degree. Every layout retains the same physical row stagger. The bottom row (`Z X C V B N M , . /`) recalls the ten memories; Shift plus a memory key captures the current chord. Memory shortcuts follow the same QWERTY focus rules. Transpose adjusts the playing register; Key sets the first chord button and keyboard key’s root, along with the selected **Scale**. Scale changes highlighting in Chromatic/Fifths and the actual chord mapping in Degrees/Passing. The Key menu offers explicit sharp/flat tonic spellings; scale notes keep their correct letter names (for example E♯ in F♯ major and C♭ in G♭ major).

The default MIDI control octave is C0–B0 (MIDI 12–23). Hover its octave on the full keyboard to reveal a red border and **Learn octave** label, click the highlighted keys, then press C in the desired octave (or another key to transpose the control layout). The twelve keys select chords by interval from that learned tonic: C major, D♭ b9, D sus2, E♭ minor, E major, F sus4, F♯ diminished, G power, A♭ augmented, A sixth, B♭ dominant seventh, B major seventh. The learned range is highlighted on the full MIDI keyboard; hover its keys to reveal the red Learn border and see each assignment. Control keys select harmony silently: Note On, Note Off, zero-velocity Note On and per-note pressure are consumed, without starting a chord or strum. Notes below the bass split go directly to the bass output; other notes outside the control octave are performance notes. Learn can be cancelled with a second click.

The first performance note anchors the chord. A second note adds or replaces a degree: C+D gives Csus2, C+F gives Csus4, C+F# gives C–E–F#, C+G gives C5 (C–G), and C+B♭ gives C7 (C–E–G–B♭). Extra notes wait while two inputs own the chord. With **Hold chord off**, releasing the second input removes its alteration. When only one key remains held, it becomes the root of a fresh chord, including a key pressed early while the previous chord was still held. Computer-keyboard chords retain that key’s original quality; MIDI notes retain their velocity and take expression from their own channel.

With **Hold chord on**, releasing either or both notes retains the complete altered chord, regardless of release order or timing. Releasing just D in C+D keeps Csus2. Re-press C to return to the base C chord; while C remains down, pressing another alteration replaces the retained one. Releases do not restart the strum or arpeggiator. Turning Hold chord off returns to the physically held inputs.

Harmony currently combines one of twelve base qualities with **one alteration**. A dominant seventh plus a semitone adds ♭9, but a tritone replaces the fifth with ♭5. Combined extensions such as 7♭9♯11, an additive ♯11 retaining the fifth, and ordinary ninth chords retaining the third are not available yet; a second note a tone above the root produces sus2 instead of add9.

Up/Down arrows cycle inversions within the original register, wrapping to root position. They do not accumulate octave changes. Input key repeats are ignored. Focus loss releases computer-keyboard notes without releasing unrelated MIDI inputs.

## Split keyboard and sustained layers

**Bass below** sets a separate bass-only boundary. Its default **Control octave** follows the bottom of the learned control octave. Drag the gold handle beneath the piano to either side of the control octave, or edit the Bass below value. Enter **Auto** to follow the control octave again. Notes below this boundary play only the selected bass MIDI channel, with sustain and note releases preserved. Control-octave notes always select qualities silently, even inside the bass range.

The Melody module’s power/bypass button enables **Key split**, which separates the remaining chord-selection keys from right-hand melody. Notes below the split choose the chord; the split note and higher notes pass through unchanged on their incoming MIDI channels. The default boundary is C4 (MIDI 60). Drag the split marker, click a key on the bottom piano when split is enabled, or click the split readout and type a note such as C4 or 60. The learned control octave is always consumed before the split is applied. Left-hand selection remains available after its keys are released.

**Play Always** in Bass holds the selected root. **Chord on Select** in Manual Strum holds the resolved chord tones, replacing Chords’ former Play Always button. They start when a right-hand key is held and stop when the last right-hand key is released. With **Hold chord** on, the accompaniment stays after release and changes to the next selected chord. Melody notes always follow their own releases. Bass Play Always and Chord on Select can be enabled without doubling shared tones; ordinary strum tails retain their note lengths. With Key split off, held performance keys gate these layers instead. New instances start with Bass Play Always off and Strum Only selected.

The full MIDI 0–127 piano shows control keys, the independent bass and right-hand splits, held input keys, sounding output notes, and selected voicing. Voice-leading paths animate above the keys, with exact note movements available on hover. Changing the split releases current notes to prevent stranded note ownership.

## Performance

The full MIDI keyboard animates each voice-leading transition: hollow starting dots above the split handles connect directly to destination keys, with dots animating along the paths during each transition. Upward, downward and stationary voices use distinct colors. The latest paths remain visible; hover the selection strip above the split handles for exact note names and semitone changes.

Changing chords preserves ringing notes. Strum and arpeggiator notes finish their scheduled lengths, including across key releases; outgoing sustained Chord-mode tones receive the current Note length. Retriggering the same pitch or exhausting voice capacity can still end an older voice. Panic and playback/routing changes stop voices.

- **Arpeggiator · Once** plays a single pattern over the selected **Strings** on chord changes. **Played** limits the strings used in each octave; upward patterns start at the low end, downward patterns at the high end. **Hold notes** sustains each strike until the next chord; otherwise **Length** controls its tail.
- **Manual Strum** uses the XY pad or **CC1 mod wheel**, automatically mapped to X. Increasing CC1 sweeps upward; decreasing it sweeps downward. X starts with an editable route to **Strumfield position**. Hover X to see its connection and click it to edit the range and curve. Drag another modulator onto the field to replace that link. Direct X still plays the strings when that route is disabled or removed; X remains both a source and a destination. Stationary controls do not retrigger. Switching modes is explicit.
- **Arpeggiator · Loop** offers up, down, up/down, played order and seeded random. Rate is in quarter-note beats (0.25 = sixteenth notes). Host tempo, gate, swing, octave range and bounded humanization are supported. It also runs while playing live with stopped transport; stop/seek transitions clear owned notes.

X crosses 3–12 virtual strings, eight by default. Y controls strike velocity by default, or gate/pressure/timbre/bend/custom CC. Touching a string directly plucks it. Learned controllers seed their initial position before sweeping, avoiding a jump from an unknown start point. Both travel directions play. **Hold chord** and CC64 sustain retain the last harmony without retaining physical input slots.

Click the chevron on the X or Y modulator tile to edit that controller directly. Choose Off, CC (automatic 7/14-bit detection), or Pitch bend, then select the CC number or bend channel. For paired CC use the MSB number 0–31; receiving its LSB (number+32) enables 14-bit resolution independently on that MIDI channel. Reset All Controllers (CC121) clears detected resolution. Saved explicit 14-bit mappings and channel filters remain compatible. X/Y MIDI Learn has been removed; use the modulator routing controls to assign other sources. Calibrate X with the Manual Strum pad’s MIN / MAX grips (25% minimum span), calibrate Y with its horizontal grips, and reverse either axis in its controller editor. Pitch-bend faders must use a channel outside the incoming MPE member zone. No hardware-specific motor feedback is emitted.

## Osmose / MPE

Use **Osmose Play / Port 1**, External MIDI mode **MPE**. Choose MPE in the protocol dropdown and select Manual Strum. Select an MPE-capable destination instrument and enable MPE in that instrument/host. Configure matching bend ranges: default member ±48 semitones, master ±2.

The first accepted note's pressure, CC74 and bend fan out to every generated voice. The second input affects harmony only. Each output voice receives its own member channel, with expression initialized before Note On, including later strummed notes. Master expression remains separate. Incoming RPN bend sensitivity and zone configuration are recognized. Released source channels cannot take expression ownership when reused.

MPE output has a single **MPE on / MPE off** switch and defaults off. The switch pulses gold when MPE input is detected while output is off. Detection recognizes announced zones, host per-note tuning/brightness, and member-channel expression; notes alone do not trigger the warning. Existing saved protocol choices (including legacy Auto) remain compatible; clicking the switch sets an explicit on/off choice. MPE output uses the lower zone by default: master channel 1, members 2–16. Upper-zone and reduced member counts are configurable. Standard single-channel MIDI is also available. In MPE mode, generated chord voices retain member-channel allocation. Direct bass-zone notes use the chosen bass channel, which is reserved from generated MPE voices while a bass zone is enabled. An engaged Y assignment overrides only its chosen expression dimension.

Actual Osmose feel, downstream patch response, and host-specific MIDI routing require the hardware acceptance checks in `docs/ACCEPTANCE.md`.

**Affect Chords** in Melody adds held melody pitch classes to the selected chord. Chord tones that match an exact held MIDI note move to another available octave within one octave of the chord register, or are omitted if no octave fits. Releasing melody restores the selected harmony; disabling the option preserves ordinary chord behavior. The option is saved with presets and defaults off.

## Discover and recall

Compatible chords are always highlighted and Roman numerals are always displayed. Select a key and scale from the menus below the chord keyboard to update them. Outside chords remain playable. Highlighting considers all chord tones, not only the root. Major, natural/harmonic minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian and major/minor pentatonic scales are available.

Eight memory keys form the fourth staggered row inside the chord keyboard module and store the current harmonic recipe: root, alteration, quality, inversion, transpose and spread. Click an empty **+** card to save, or Shift-click a card to save or replace it. Saving requires a valid current chord. Shift-click and Shift plus a memory key remain capture shortcuts. The interface confirms a save only after the stored slot updates; an identical chord reports that it is already stored, and an unconfirmed request asks you to try again. Click a filled card to recall it into the current performance mode. Recall remains available for strumming until a new performance input replaces it. Empty cards have a dashed outline. Hover a filled card to reveal its × delete button. Drag a filled card onto an empty card to move it, or onto another filled card to swap the two. Release outside the cards to cancel a drag. Slots persist with project state and host presets.

Standard MIDI can route bass and upper voices to separate channels. MPE retains its member-channel allocator.

The eight slots are a manual progression sketchpad, not a step sequencer. MIDI drag-out, constrained chord search and a separate previous/current comparison view are follow-ons.

## Selection and modulation

**Manual Strum** places a three-way selector above the strings: **Strum Only → Root on Select → Chord on Select**. Strum Only waits for a strum; Root on Select sounds the transposed root immediately when choosing a chord, including in Manual Strum. It follows Note length and normal release/sustain behavior; Auto Strum and the first arp step do not double it. Chord on Select uses the sustained chord layer described above. Existing Root on Select and chord Play Always settings retain their saved behavior.

The full-width **Modulation** strip between the upper modules and keyboard meters pitch wheel, mod wheel (CC1), expression (CC11), breath (CC2), pressure, timbre (CC74), final note velocity after contour and humanization, and raw strum X/Y. MIDI expression meters use the latest incoming value across channels. Pitch fills outward from its neutral center. The Velocity meter updates before each generated note is sent; velocity routes to note length and gate use that note’s final velocity. Routes that shape velocity itself use raw input velocity to avoid feedback. **Drag a source meter onto a control** to link it. Source cards highlight on hover; dragging shows eligible destinations, a cable and the proposed link. Clicking a meter opens its route settings; clicking the selected meter again returns to the chordboard. The selected meter pulses with a strong border, and one inset dot marks each assigned route. Drop elsewhere or press Escape during the drag to cancel. Hovering a linked source highlights its visible destinations.

A successful drop slides that link’s range and curve editor into the Chords module; Performance remains usable. Close it with × or Escape. Hovering a modulator shows clickable destination chips below it, including disabled links, and wires leave the top or bottom edge toward each destination. Dropping onto an already linked destination reuses the effective route and preserves its range and curve; the preview says when the drop will replace its source. Repeating the same link opens it without adding duplicates. MIDI output controls are always visible in three equal-width modules below the keyboard; open a controller editor to drop onto its local controls. The editor shows assigned destinations as chips with **+ Add route**, plus categorized source and destination menus. The separate Routes button and X/Y controller popovers are removed. Existing controller assignments are preserved. Click a destination overlay’s **×** to show **Are you sure?**; only its check mark removes the route.

Each slot has an enable switch, source, destination, and a compact interactive graph. Drag the left/right endpoints vertically to set minimum/maximum; drag the center node vertically to bend the curve. Destination readouts use musical units (for example, Strings 3–12 or Voicing Close–Drop 3). Minimum greater than maximum reverses the mapping. **Linear ↺** resets the curve. The small live dot and input/output readout show the current modulation.

Sources can drive multiple destinations across performance, voicing, expression and MIDI-output controls. Routes replace the destination within their range; when several routes target the same parameter, the highest-numbered enabled route wins. Routed controls show teal live values and their driving source. Attempts to edit them identify the controlling route; disable or remove it to restore manual control. Disabling a route restores that base setting (or recalled voicing). Routes and their settings save with the host project and can be automated. Existing X/Y MIDI assignments remain stored alongside these routes. Touch-range X/Y minimum and maximum are not modulation destinations; their grips remain available for direct calibration. Saved routes to those four bounds are inactive. Strum X / sweep remains routable without feeding its result back into modulation sources.

## Interface

The preferred initial window is 10% larger than the base artwork dimensions (subject to saved sizing and screen fit). The 1871 × 866 interface balances the chord keyboard and voicing controls with the performance surface. Manual Strum includes Flattery’s shared graph zoom icon, which grows the strum field over the plugin body below the title header. The module edge uses the shared quarter-second quintic slide: performance width and position interpolate while the chord surfaces shrink, matching Composure’s envelope expansion. The inward icon returns the field. Both sizes preserve identical graph insets and handle-label clearances, with the zoom button anchored at the top-right and Latch directly to its left. Both plugins draw these icons from `pleasant-ui/src/draw/icons.rs::Draw::graph_zoom_button`. Arpeggiator closes an expanded field. Hover the keyboard voicing for a border and click to cycle **Close / Open / Wide / Drop 2 / Drop 3**. The voice-leading selection strip sits above the split handles and keeps its original dimensions; paths descend from the previous chord directly to the keyboard keys. Nearest and Furthest show a persistent purple border and mode label; click to cycle **Nearest / Furthest / Off**. Bass stays left-aligned, Melody right-aligned, and the purple Chord module sits immediately left of Melody. Melody dims when bypassed. Bass, Chords and Melody place all controls in aligned buttons with small rounded corners inline with their titles; switching to MPE keeps those dimensions and omits separate bend-range controls. Gold Key, Scale and layout controls share the main CHORDS header with a dark grey segmented transpose control above the QWERTY board. Latch sits beside the memory row as a larger gold-bordered button with an explicit on/off state. Dropdowns dismiss by choosing an option, clicking outside or pressing Escape. Chord on Select uses the Chords module’s purple highlight. Buttons throughout the editor use Damian’s shared 5 px corner radius, with selection accents drawn as rounded backgrounds offset 2 px below the opaque button face. The Performance modes share its header, with Host/Manual tempo and BPM in the plugin header. Channel selectors remain in the keyboard submodule headers. Bass and Melody both have bypass buttons and **Learn Split**: play a MIDI note or click a key to set a boundary. Crossed learned boundaries swap, and dragging preserves one chord key between Bass and Melody. Bass bypass disables its direct routing and Play Always layer. Split handles use the shared EQ nodes and selection rings. Voice-leading animation descends from previous notes toward the new notes at the keyboard, whose selected voicing has a yellow fill and border. Compact inversion controls sit above the keyboard; the full keyboard below shows the resulting voicing. Spread affects playback voicing. The Quality panel is removed; existing quality parameters and MIDI control-octave behavior remain available. **Set control octave** sets the control octave. Computer-keyboard playing is always enabled while focused; the former enable control is removed.

Arpeggiator combines **Once** and **Loop** on one page with the same seven direction buttons: Up, Down, Up / Down, Up 3rds, Down 3rds, As played, and Random. A single **Rate** controls the time between notes in both modes. Toggle **Tempo sync** for beat divisions or **Free · ms** for milliseconds. Octaves, Swing and Humanize remain shared. Loop uses Gate; Once uses Hold notes and Length. The strings display covers the pitch range, animates each strike, and keeps sounding notes highlighted. Velocity appears on both playback pages; Length and Strings appear in Once and Manual Strum, and Played appears in Once. There is no performance settings page. Each modulation route has its own input-to-output **Contour**: drag the graph to tilt its range, drag the endpoints to set its bounds, or drag the center node to bend its response. Horizontal and vertical contour drags lock to their initial axis; Shift makes fine adjustments. Reversed ranges work on every route. Existing route parameter IDs and shapes are preserved.

Modulators form a horizontal row above the keyboard; both span the narrower editor width. The output protocol and MIDI controls live in the wide, single-row Bass, Chord and Melody submodules above the keyboard. Menus and controller popovers have explicit close controls. Clicking a modulator opens its routing editor; clicking it again closes it. While open, its meter and visible connections continue pulsing even when the pointer leaves. Route chips use inset stroke icons: X asks for confirmation and the check removes the link. Manual CC assignments accept any input channel; MIDI Learn locks the assignment to the learned channel. The field’s Y axis controls velocity by default; the external Y controller starts unassigned. CC11 (Expression) changes velocity only when explicitly mapped or routed. Existing saved controller assignments are preserved. The X and Y MIN / MAX grips retain direct range calibration on the strum surface. In Manual Strum, the touch-icon **Latch** control plays the strum field on pointer hover so a mouse or trackpad does not need to click and hold.

The compact Chords module uses 66 × 78 px tiles with the original row stagger. The four tile rows are vertically centered beneath the header, and the narrower Chords module reduces the full editor and keyboard width by 128 px. Performance retains room for its full control rows; Velocity lives directly on each playback page, and Keyboard is shortened to match the single-row controls. The gap above the Z memory bank is 10 px; row gaps are 8 px. Module padding, performance graph height and piano key height are tightened without increasing text sizes. The default editor opens at native scale. Playing shortcuts retain their physical keys across all four layouts. Hover memories, meters or controls for contextual detail. The interface uses shared colors, appearance preferences and controls. Key depression, pressure glow, string pulses, gesture trails and expression meters follow engine state; visuals never control MIDI timing. The strum field, trails, timing and calibration behavior are preserved. Continuous controls can be dragged from anywhere in their bounds or scrolled; click a value without dragging for text entry (Shift-drag makes fine adjustments). Values display meaningful units such as percentages, milliseconds and semitones, and typed input accepts the displayed units. On/off settings are buttons.

Use the host's preset and project features to save custom setups. Host state persists mappings, chord memories and editor settings. Runtime held notes are never saved. Appearance follows the application's preference file.

## Build and install

From the parent `audio-plugins` workspace:

```sh
cargo check -p chordboard
cargo test -p chordboard --quiet
cargo clippy -p chordboard
chordboard/scripts/install.sh
```

The installer rebuilds, bundles, signs and verifies both Chordboard formats in the user's plugin directories. Running the bundler alone does not install them.

This repository is a full-history subtree at `chordboard/`. Standalone checkout builds need the sibling Pleasant libraries and the parent workspace's patched NIH-plug/baseview/Vizia sources under `Damian Channel Strip/vendor`, matching existing plugins. Prefer the integrated workspace; subtree export does not carry those dependencies or the parent installer.

Interactive native preview (uses the real plugin with a dummy backend by default):

```sh
cargo run -p chordboard --features ui-preview --bin chordboard-preview
```

Click the mode controls and protocol dropdown to change performance mode and output protocol. This uses NIH-plug's standalone wrapper, including parameter updates and the real processing loop. The default dummy backend opens no audio/MIDI devices, so MIDI Learn requires an explicitly selected MIDI-capable backend (`-- --help` lists options). On macOS it requires a graphical session and OpenGL access. No REAPER automation is used.

## Architecture and reference

`harmony.rs` resolves fixed-capacity voicings. `engine/` owns input admission, lifecycle, timing, controllers and MPE. `lib.rs` adapts sample-timed NIH-plug events. `bridge.rs` provides bounded lock-free UI queues; UI overflow requests Panic rather than losing releases. `params.rs` defines stable host IDs and versioned saved fields. `ui/` renders and handles focused input.

All audio-thread storage is bounded: two inputs, fifteen generated voices, sixty-four pending strikes, a 512-event outgoing batch and bounded GUI queues. An overflowing batch is discarded atomically and previously sounding notes are released; a new performance input resumes playback. Note Off ownership is independent of the pending-strike queue. Channel exhaustion ends the oldest voice before reuse.

Original software inspired by the interaction model of Benjamin Poilvé's [minichord](https://github.com/BenjaminPoilve/minichord); firmware was consulted as a reference, not copied or linked. Discovery features were inspired by KawaChord2. Chordboard does not include their branding, firmware, presets or artwork. GPL-3.0-or-later, consistent with the shared project libraries.

Y destination, custom CC and Reverse Y share the Y controller editor; Reverse X is in the X controller editor. Compact vertical pressure, timbre and bend meters sit at bottom right. Control-octave Learn is on the main interface. Transpose buttons in Chordboard step by −12, −1, +1 or +12 semitones, with Reset returning to zero. Escape closes an open menu or popover and cancels armed memory saving.

Playback pages use the shared quarter-second slide animation. QWERTY playing is always enabled while the editor is focused, and key labels stay visible. The chord-name readout beside the memory cards remains visible after release. Swing supports −75% to +75%, reversing the long/short step pattern below zero.

HOST SYNC follows DAW tempo (120 BPM fallback if unavailable); disable it to set manual BPM. The arpeggiator’s Tempo sync toggle selects beat divisions instead of milliseconds for both Once and Loop, using the same tempo source.

The arpeggiator keeps its chord-triggered clock across control edits, output routing, inversion commands, key-release promotion, transport start and performance-mode changes. Selecting a new root or recalling a chord establishes a fresh start. Octave range and pattern edits apply at the end of the current pattern cycle; rate and swing edits apply at the next two-step boundary. Fractional sample intervals carry forward to avoid cumulative rounding drift. Gate, contour and humanization do not reset the clock.

HOST SYNC currently follows BPM only, without a time-signature selector or DAW beat/bar-position alignment. Chord quality/filter edits can change the number of notes and therefore the length of a melodic cycle without restarting the rhythmic clock. Pattern cycles need not match bar lengths. Tempo edits apply to newly scheduled intervals, so tempo automation can still diverge from the DAW beat grid. Stop/seek and input-split changes retain their all-notes-off behavior.

### Smart voice leading

Click the bordered voice-leading diagram to cycle **Nearest resolution → Furthest dominant resolution → Off** (default). Nearest chooses the inversion and octave placement with the least ordered voice movement from the previous chord. Furthest dominant favors the most movement for major/dominant chords resolving down a fifth to major or minor, and uses nearest movement elsewhere. Candidates stay within one octave of the requested voicing and retain its chord tones; note filters apply afterward.

The first chord uses the requested inversion/register. Repeated chords retain their resolved voicing across note releases. Panic/reset clears the history; changing inversion, transpose, spread, playback mode, or voice-leading mode starts a fresh voicing. The mode is saved and automatable as `voice_leading`; existing presets default to Off. **Interlock** sits at the right of the voice-leading row and works with every voice-leading mode: Avoid chords softens and shortens arp strikes that land on a chord strike, Match chords accents them. It is saved and automatable as `interlock`. An Arp sequencer page can override it; otherwise the page follows this global setting.

Chord quality uses a clickable symbol grid, including sus2, sus4 and ♭9, with animated hover feedback. Its highlight follows the engine’s current control-octave selection. Voice leading and spread use direct-choice buttons.

## Live values and layout

Chords and Performance sit above a full-width modulation row. The full MIDI keyboard has voice-leading paths and split handles above its keys, with equal-width Bass, Chords and Melody settings below. Transpose has semitone/octave steps and an explicit Reset button that resets the saved base value; active modulation continues to override that base. Routed button choices show a teal live highlight, and sliders retain the saved base marker.

Module headers follow Pleasant UI typography, including the shared 24-unit title. Chordboard retains its 1621 × 840 design size. Fresh windows fit within 90% of screen width with room for host chrome; saved instance sizes, remembered user sizes and manual resizing override this initial fit. The bottom-right handle resizes independently of the default screen allowance. Older presets gain the default X route in a free slot while preserving existing links. A full legacy matrix keeps its original direct X behavior.

The current held or latched chord has a persistent pulsing highlight on the chordboard, including chords played over MIDI or recalled from memory. Split-learning key previews use gold for Bass and teal for Melody. Click any modulator and select a route to edit that route’s Contour.

## Sequencer

Use the **Play** / **Sequencer** tabs in the plugin header to switch pages, and the power button beside them to enable or bypass sequencer playback. The sequencer runs independent Arp, Chords, and Harmony timelines. Performance contains only Arpeggiator and Manual Strum; selecting either page does not switch sequencing off. The original Play mode parameter retains its range for existing host automation. The bypass button beside **STRUMFIELD** enables manual strumming alongside sequencing and plays the current sequenced harmony. Bypassing it releases manual strum notes while sequencer notes continue. Audio remains an unchanged stereo passthrough.

On the Sequencer page the keyboard module becomes the pattern editor, the Chords module becomes the Arpeggiator with its step editor, and the Strumfield stays unchanged. Three rows show all 32 steps across the module. Length sits directly below each row. Lock lengths starts enabled and links edits across the three currently edited patterns; switch it off for independent lengths. The loop strip above the rows auditions part of the selected lane's edited page: click a step to loop it, or drag to loop adjacent steps. **Loop all** returns to the full pattern. The loop is never saved and ends when you leave the Sequencer page or select another lane or page. Each lane has eight named pages, its own length and loop start; Chords and Harmony also have their own rate. New patterns default to one step, preserving continuous arpeggiator traversal. The row Length sliders resize currently edited pages; darkened cells beyond each endpoint are inactive. A filled page button is the page being edited; an underline marks the playing page, and a gold dot marks a pending selection. Clicking a numbered page button selects it for editing and requests playback for that lane at its next step boundary. Drag a modulator onto the row’s **1–8** buttons as a unit to control its playing page. Use **Play pattern** in the Pattern inspector to request the editing page again. Routine edits preserve the running phase; restoring a preset resets lane ownership and timing.

While the sequencer is open, expression step-edit buttons appear above their matching modulators: Bend above Pitch, Pressure above Press, Timbre above Timbre, and Velocity above Vel. CC tracks assigned to Mod (CC1), Expression (CC11), or Breath (CC2) also get a button above that modulator. Click one to edit that value across steps; click it again to return to Steps. Click a note step to toggle its hit/rest state and open its inspector; right-click edits without toggling. Step controls include gate/tie, octave, probability, ratchets, and microtiming. Velocity above Vel edits step velocity relative to live input. Arp steps choose a chord tone or follow the arpeggiator ordering. Rest consumes timeline time. The Ghosts tool fills resting steps at lower velocity. Interlock (see voice leading) softens or accents arp strikes coincident with chord strikes.

**Harmony** is an adjustment timeline: drag on empty space to draw a box, click the box to edit its root offset, quality, inversion, and voicing, and drag its right edge (or edit Box length) to resize. Delete box removes it. Each adjustment applies only inside its span; gaps return to the live chord. Chords is the note-trigger lane, while Harmony changes the underlying recipe used by note lanes. Adjustments remain relative to the latest played or recalled chord and never accumulate. Existing held recipes become explicit spans when first edited with the box tool.

Page destinations appended to the modulation matrix control effective playback while manual choices retain their base selections. Page requests take effect at each lane's next step boundary without restarting its phase; shorter loop ranges wrap the existing phase.

Expression steps add signed raw controller values to live pressure, timbre, and the pattern’s four CC tracks. Pitch-bend offsets use semitones and the configured output bend range. Results clamp to legal output values. Neutral CC tracks with no received baseline send nothing. Protocol, sustain, bank-select, and paired high-resolution CC assignments are reserved. MPE gives generated voices independent channels; regular MIDI expression is channel-wide, so simultaneous voices sharing a channel share its current expression.

Host playback runs the clocks. With transport stopped, performance input or Hold chord gates the local clock at the selected host/manual tempo. Stop, seek, panic, and preset replacement release owned notes. Negative microtiming uses lookahead during steady playback; the first step and a page/edit request arriving after the lookahead horizon are clipped at the next boundary rather than played retrospectively. Existing Once and Loop behavior remains available.


### Sequencer control hierarchy

The header power button enables sequencing directly from the editor. Basic controls are note-step on/off, velocity, gate/tie, octave, note selection (Arp), pattern length/rate, pattern enable, and playback selection. Harmony replaces hit/rest controls with drawable adjustment boxes. Arp is teal, Chords/Harmony purple, and Bass gold, matching the keyboard.

The inspector has direct **Step** and **Pattern** tabs. The inspector sits inside the Arpeggiator module. Expression editing lives above the matching modulators. There is no Advanced gate or separate More page. Pattern owns loop start, lane playback controls, and Ghosts; non-Arp lanes also expose their rate here. Loop end always follows Length. Arp order and rate live in the Arpeggiator module, along with the Arp page's Interlock override.

Choose Gate, Chance, Octave, Timing, or Repeats above the grid, or an expression selector above its modulator, to turn note cells into full-cell vertical sliders. Drag across cells to paint values; the wheel adjusts one value; Cmd-click in a value mode resets it. Rest/hit status stays unchanged while editing values. Click a numeric Step value to select its cell-edit mode. Double-click the numeric value for exact text entry. Choose **Steps** to return to hit/rest editing. Right-click selects a step without toggling it, and selecting another step preserves the inspector tab.

The Arpeggiator always uses the same Once/Loop layout, including ordering diagrams, string preview, musical rate, octave range, humanization, swing, and output controls. It appears only on the Sequencer page. The Step and Pattern controls sit alongside the arpeggiator settings, using the full module width. Ordering and rate are shared: the Arp lane steps at the arpeggiator rate and uses its ordering, whether or not the sequencer is bypassed. The Interlock button sets an override for the edited Arp page: Global follows the voice-leading row's Interlock, or choose Off, Avoid chords, or Match chords for that page. Step note and expression settings remain in the sequencer.

**Latch:** click the tile or tap Shift to toggle. Holding Shift temporarily inverts the current latch state and restores it on release (250 ms hold threshold). Shift used with another key remains a modifier and restores the prior latch state; focus loss also restores it. Text entry does not trigger the shortcut.
