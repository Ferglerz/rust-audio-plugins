# Helpinstill Roadmaster 64

The 1979 Roadmaster is an acoustic spinet with a folding keybed, two strings per course in the manufacturer's specification, a working soundboard, and magnetic pickups. The original 64 has a single output jack to the left of the keyboard. Its A1–C7 compass removes the bottom and top octaves of an 88-note piano. A technician who opened a 64 identifies three installed strips covering bass, middle and treble. The three individual line outputs on that particular restoration were added by the shop. The Mark II 88 has a separately documented breakout box and requires its own scale. [Helpinstill's June 1979 specification](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Audio/Mix-Magazine/MI/MI-1980-01-02.pdf) · [Mike Moran's 1980 playing test, p. 57](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/International-Musician-%26-Recording-World/International-Musician-1980-05.pdf) · [Chicago Electric Piano's 64 rewiring](https://chicagoelectricpiano.com/helpinstill/unprecedented-versatility-the-3-output-winter-white-helpinstill/) · [Mark II 88 restoration](https://chicagoelectricpiano.com/helpinstill/an-in-depth-look-at-the-helpinstill-roadmaster-mark-ii/)

## From hammer to jack and soundboard

A compression-only felt law drives the struck strings and reacts on the moving hammer. Each slightly detuned string has dispersive transverse modes. One end follows the moving bridge. For normalized distance `u` from the fixed end, the string displacement is `u × bridge + Σ qₙ sin(nπu)`. This gives the joint mass term `m_string × (−1)ⁿ⁺¹ / (nπ)` between each string partial and its local bridge motion. The three board modes receive the reaction and return an acceleration to every course. Solving the remaining three-by-three mass matrix includes both directions of transfer. A held silent course therefore moves when another string drives the board. The shared FIR decimator preserves time alignment between the three electrical taps and the board surface velocity.

Helpinstill's patent describes a flexible, through-thickness magnet with a preferred 12.7 mm face width, 3.175 mm thickness and 300 turns of 36 AWG wire. It places the bar between the frame and strings, with the sensing face parallel to the string plane. Each model strip integrates displacement along the string over that face, then differentiates flux linkage. The three gaps can change independently in millimetres. The point along the string controls which partials cancel. The recording voltage depends on the coil circuit and receiver. [Helpinstill patent US4212220A, figs. 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4212220A/en)

The loaded jack uses three estimated coil sources, 10 kΩ isolation resistors and a 1 MΩ receiver with 470 pF of cable capacitance. Three assumed bar lengths give roughly 239–337 Ω winding resistance at 20 °C. Coil inductance, capacitance, relative polarity, mono summing topology, gain and loaded response still need measurements on an original 64. The board output is an area-weighted surface velocity in m/s. Converting it to acoustic pressure requires the cabinet geometry, radiation transfer and microphone position. Prototype levels are provisional.

Native scale anchors at A1, C3, C4, C6 and C7 interpolate length, tension, felt mass and compliance, strike and sensor position, and inharmonicity. The C3/C4/C6 pilot lengths are 0.90/0.56/0.14 m. The three board resonances at 105/181/313 Hz and their modal masses, Q and attachment factors are working estimates. The approximate section transitions at F♯3 and E♭5 need a physical trace. Extended notes carry the nearest native string mass, pickup geometry and hammer, with the tension adjusted for their own pitch. An unplayed virtual string has zero mechanical participation. The live approximation retains three partials per strand in silent native courses, and promotes a struck course to its full modeled spectrum. The offline reference retains the complete pilot spectrum in all three keys.

## Independent observations

The workshop's early 64 had brass tacks in the felt hammers. This changes the strike and makes the restoration unsuitable as a stock felt calibration. Vongon's 64 recording identifies the path through a Paragraphs effect into an interface, with a microphone mix for the feedback passage. During its opening section, mixed tones and processing prevent isolation of the original sensor response. [Tack-hammer 64](https://chicagoelectricpiano.com/blogs/news/helpinstill-roadmaster) · [Vongon's signal chain and chapter times](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tmgKfP9dHOw)

A second film shows the player unfolding a Roadmaster and playing the keyboard. At 15.586 and 19.385 s two C3 windows have 97% and 93% of their 70–1550 Hz power close to harmonics. The strongest component is the second harmonic. Its level relative to the fundamental is +5.15/+6.11 dB. The fourth is −24.15/−30.92 dB. At a 0.22-length sensor position the three-course probe gives +2.97 and −9.85 dB. Its board tap gives −16.71 and −37.14 dB. The two modeled sources predict different spectra from the same string. The clip supplies a performance reference with unknown microphone, pickup mix, model serial number, hammer velocity and pedal state. Its board and electrical responses cannot be fitted separately. [Owner's demonstration, 15–22 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkAAf13PzSQ)

Holding the same hammer, string and board motion fixed, a sweep from 0.18 to 0.32 of the string length puts the deepest modeled fourth-partial dip near 0.26. At that position C3 H2/H3/H4 are +1.91/−22.53/−19.09 dB relative to the fundamental. The fourth moves toward the filmed signal while the second falls farther away and the third remains weak. The stock model keeps the provisional 0.22 position. A stock-64 installation trace and simultaneous direct/microphone recording would constrain the location and source mixture independently.

At pedal down the C3 strike appears in the other modeled strips at −15.9 and −62.0 dB RMS relative to the bass strip in the reference probe. Locking the board removes both cross-course outputs and the board velocity. Moving the magnetic bars 1 mm farther reduces their RMS by 2.48 dB and preserves every sample of the board output. These values test the equations and gap direction. The hardware still needs a simultaneous DI, fixed mic and preferably a bridge velocity measurement to establish the size of the transfer.

Across all 64 native keys and eight-note chords at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, the largest full-velocity jack peak is 22.235 mV, on MIDI 51/58/63/67/70/74/77/79. The largest board velocity is 43.364 mm/s, on MIDI 45/52/57/61/64/68/71/73. The plugin scales board velocity by 0.5127 for comparable monitor headroom. At 48 kHz a sustained middle C costs about 25% of a core on the test Mac and the eight-note chord about 36%. Each silent native course remains mechanically coupled to the board. MIDI All Sound Off clears the course and board motion together through a 4 ms output taper and retains the physical damper-pedal position. A note-specific choke damps only that course and then restores its passive coupling.

## Reproduction

From the workspace root, the following renders the three sensor EMFs and the board velocity as four-channel float WAVs with unchanged units. A second example exercises the live keyboard.

```sh
cargo run -p roadmaster --release --example probe
cargo run -p roadmaster --release --example live
cargo run -p roadmaster --release --example compass -- 48000
cargo test -p roadmaster --quiet
python3 roadmaster/research/compare.py target/roadmaster-proof
```

The comparison runs with NumPy, SciPy and SoundFile. To reproduce the filmed C3 comparison, decode the owner clip to a 44.1 kHz mono WAV named `luthier.wav` in the same output directory. The comparison then writes a level-matched four-part listening file, `c3-owner-owner-sensor-board.wav`, containing the two filmed C3 windows followed by the modeled bass sensor and soundboard.
