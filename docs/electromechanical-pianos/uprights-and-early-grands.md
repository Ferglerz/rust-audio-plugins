# Helpinstill, Baldwin, Neo-Bechstein and Storytone

These four instruments can use a dispersive string solver. They need four different models of how motion reaches the listener. A Roadmaster soundboard loads the strings even at its electrical output. The Baldwin patent couples strings through a pressure-sensitive rail. The Neo-Bechstein groups strings under a magnetic sensor. The Storytone has individual note pickups and a loudspeaker labyrinth. Prioritize the two historical amplifier studies for circuit work. Start instrument voicing on whichever specified specimen supplies repeatable notes and an identifiable output path.

## Helpinstill Roadmaster

Target a stock **Roadmaster 64** first. Give the 88 its own scale when a specimen is available. The maker's advertisement lists a soundboard, dual stringing, permanently installed sensors and both sizes. Chicago Electric Piano Company photographed a 64 under repair with brass tacks added to the hammers. Its action photographs help reconstruct the keybed, but the modification changes attack. Helpinstill's upright-sensor patent gives a magnetic construction and mounting rationale. The current Model 125 manual describes a separate retrofit product. [Helpinstill advertisement, Musician, July 1981](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Musician/1980/1981/Musician-1981-07.pdf) · [Workshop record of the 64](https://chicagoelectricpiano.com/blogs/news/helpinstill-roadmaster) · [Charles Helpinstill, US4212220A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4212220A/en) · [Model 125 manual](https://helpinstill.com/125manual.pdf)

1. Draw one key from escapement and hammer through its unisons, dampers, bridge and board. Record the lowest/highest pitch, string count and scale breaks. Mark the placement and span of each installed sensor relative to each string, connector wiring, section gains and load. Select pilot notes crossing a stringing break and a sensor boundary.
2. Reuse the CP-80 modal string/contact method with upright action and felt measured for the Roadmaster. Add a passive bridge admittance fitted to board mobility. Drive the board from string reaction and feed bridge movement back into the strings. Reconstruct the signed magnetic readout over each sensor's spatial aperture. Set the electrical mix from actual wiring. The model should expose string, board and pickup observations independently.
3. Simultaneously capture the stock output, a fixed close mic and, if practical, bridge motion. Include pedal-up and pedal-down decays, a held silent key excited by another, and notes on either side of each sensor boundary. Use the mic/bridge measurement to fit mechanical loss and cross-coupling, then fit sensor geometry and load against the line signal. A different mic position should change its captured spectrum while leaving the line response stable.

**Prototype.** The [Roadmaster 64 report](../../roadmaster/README.md) now includes the manufacturer’s A1–C7 compass, three confirmed sensor bands, a two-way string and soundboard solver, C3/C4/C6 sensor and board renders and two measured C3 performance windows. The 64’s exact bar locations, electrical combining, board admittance and original felt regulation remain open. The Mark II 88 needs its own scale and breakout-box trace.

## Baldwin Electro-Piano / Electropiano

Use the **88-note classroom Electropiano** as the initial target. The US trade investigation identifies 64- and 88-note production models. The Baldwin specifications reproduced in LeCroy's study list a vertical action, felt-covered birch hammers, pressure-sensitive transducers, a mechanical sustain pedal, electronic soft pedal and solid-state amplification. Baldwin's patent shows an acoustically conducting rail with piezo elements and several possible terminations. This is good evidence for which force to model. The specimen still needs to identify its rail layout and circuit revision. [USITC report 721, appendix p. A-60](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/337/pub721.pdf) · [LeCroy, Baldwin specifications, printed p. 140](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark%3A/67531/metadc662988/m2/1/high_res_d/1002773114-LeCroy.pdf) · [Baldwin patent US3519721A, figs. 1–5](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3519721A/en)

1. Identify the model and plate serial, photograph the under-string rail and count elements, their wiring and strings per bearing. Document which afterlengths touch damping material. Measure relative transfers from a light impulse at three rail locations to the pickup output with the strings damped. This distinguishes local force sensitivity from propagation along the rail.
2. Build one bass, one transition and one treble note with measured lengths, winding, felts and damper timing. Project their bearing reactions into a short coupled rail model. Read strain or force at actual element positions and solve the piezo capacitances into the measured preamp load. Share the charge-source primitive with Yamaha. Give Baldwin its own rail and input network.
3. Trace the panel, amplifier and soft-pedal wiring on the selected unit. For identical hammer motion, compare pickup, electrical output, and speaker-mic response at both pedal positions. Use same-note restrikes and held intervals to check shared-rail decay and crosstalk. Reserve at least one pair of nonadjacent notes for a blind prediction of transfer across the rail.

The sources disagree on the pickup. The USITC groups Baldwin with magnetic instruments on p. A-2, while its Baldwin-specific entry on A-60 establishes only that the sensor is non-electrostatic. Figure 3 shows the exterior. LeCroy's model specifications and the builders' patent make pressure sensing the better first hypothesis. Technicians also describe a change from multi-string unisons to one string per note during production. Inspect the element and rail, measure unloaded impedance versus frequency and correlate the output with local rail motion before fixing the pickup model. If a particular specimen has coils instead, document their position, polarity and loaded response and use a magnetic readout for that build. [USITC report](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/337/pub721.pdf) · [Baldwin school-piano service accounts, posts 8–9](https://my.ptg.org/communities/community-home/digestviewer/viewthread?CommunityKey=06a50b7b-c49f-4c2a-b32b-ef78b4ed51cc&GroupId=1165&MID=365502&tab=digestviewer)

**Playable implementation.** The 88-key classroom model includes a felt hammer, wound and plain courses, two pressure-sensing rail sections and independently observable low and high pickup leads. A played note can set adjacent strings moving through the rail. At identical hammer velocity its electronic soft pedal halves the output amplitude. Across four keys reserved from the hammer and shim fit, the median harmonic-level error was 10.17 dB. The attacks are faster than either filmed owner attack. The production rail geometry and original pedal attenuation still need measurement. The separate 64-key variant needs its own compass and transducer survey. [Instrument, evidence and reproduction](../../baldwin-electropiano/README.md)

## Neo-Bechstein

Use the **Siemens ELV413 configuration** as the electronics target. Bechstein describes the soundboardless grand, microhammers and pickups serving groups of five strings. Nernst's drawings show ways of transferring momentum to the microhammer and applying or lifting extra damping. R. Wittig traced and tested the Vienna museum amplifier, published its circuit in figure 8 and measured its frequency response in figure 10. The work identifies a variable capacitor operated by the swell pedal and the loudspeaker load. It supports an amplifier reconstruction even before a keyboard can be measured. [Bechstein's history](https://www.bechstein.com/en/die-welt-von-bechstein/tradition/1930-1945/) · [Nernst patent US1988564A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1988564A/en) · [Wittig, ELV413 examination, Funkamateur 2/2007, pp. 153–155](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Funkschau/FunkAmateur-2007-02.pdf)

1. Select a documented piano/amp pairing. Trace the actual key-to-microhammer linkage and identify the damper and pedal modes. Record speaking lengths, wire, hammer mass or dimensions, and the position and grouping of the coils. Prototype a note in the middle of one group plus two notes on opposite sides of its edge. Drive a low-mass striker from the primary action through the observed stop or spring. Integrate the actual striker/string contact and the sustained string motion separately.
2. Sum the strings' flux changes with the signs and spatial weights for each coil. Fit its electrical load. Build the ELV413 input, swell capacitance, preamp and transformer-coupled push-pull stage. Compute terminal voltage into the reported 6 Ω load and compare a low-level sweep with figure 10, including the dip near 10 kHz. Measure the cabinet transfer separately for audience-position comparisons. Retain rebuilt-component values with their provenance. [Wittig, figs. 8 and 10 and technical data](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/INTERNATIONAL/Funkschau/FunkAmateur-2007-02.pdf)
3. Verify onset delay and dynamic range at soft strikes, decay with added damping engaged/lifted where fitted, shared-coil output for intervals, and swell at fixed key velocity. With only amplified recordings, hold hammer mass and coil sensitivity provisional. The mechanical acceptance recording needs an identified unit and dry musical input to the ELV413 or simultaneous motion and cabinet measurements.

The Carl Bechstein Stiftung describes serial 138994 with single strings in the outer registers and unisons in the middle. The two highest sensor groups each cover four courses. Use these as explicit boundary cases if that instrument is chosen. Confirm its own pedal linkages and paired amplifier before applying the Vienna circuit and pedal law. [Foundation collection, serial 138994](https://www.carl-bechstein-stiftung.de/instrumentensammlung/details/neo-bechstein-138994/)

## Story & Clark / RCA Storytone

Target the **1939–early 1940s electric spinet**. The National Music Museum documents the strings, electromagnetic pickups, absence of a soundboard, and separate soft, sustain and swell pedals. The period service article supplies the pickup layout, a voicing procedure using string magnetization, the amplifier drawing and the acoustic labyrinth. The factory-style note pickup, pedal circuit and speaker enclosure are each testable. [National Music Museum, object 15733](https://emuseum.nmmusd.org/objects/80661/storytone) · [Period service diagrams, Radio-Craft, February 1940, printed pp. 469 and 490 onward](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Radio-Craft/1940s/Radio-Craft-1940-02.pdf#page=23)

1. Select an electric specimen, photograph its plate and chassis, then map the upright action, string courses, pickup plates and pedals. Model each hammer, damper and string course with its own scale. Map the eight pickup plates and register a signed sensing weight per note, accounting for magnetization and coil orientation, and solve the series sections under the amplifier's input load. Sample adjacent notes across a plate boundary. Fit velocity dependence at the pickup terminal before setting amplifier gain.
2. Transcribe the piano input path, level controls, swell and transformer/tube output from the service drawing for the matched chassis. Compare DC operating points, injected sweeps, harmonic growth and pedal law against the drawing and/or a measured unit. Separate the loudspeaker's direct response from the labyrinth contribution with close measurements at each opening. Model their relative delay, polarity and level, then predict a third mic position.
3. Validate soft, sustain and swell separately. Hold one ringing note and move swell to isolate the electrical change. Record the same struck interval with and without damper lift for the mechanical test. Compare release, level and partial balance at both the pickup and acoustic output. Test any proposed speaker-to-string feedback by varying amplifier level while observing string decay. Include feedback only if that experiment identifies it.

**Implemented.** The playable Storytone now covers A0–C8 with eight accessible pickup-plate outputs, independent soft/sustain/swell controls and a traced RCA amplifier. The electrical solver matched its independent ngspice reconstruction. With its output ratio fitted to the service rating, it produced 15.90 W into 15 Ω at 2.00% harmonic distortion. A5 and D6 informed one smooth upper-register voicing fit. Withheld B5 still has 8–12 dB excess second harmonic and 12–26 dB excess third harmonic relative to the owner recording. Exact stringing, plate assignments, input impedance, damper timing, and the direct/labyrinth radiation remain specimen measurements.
