# Forks, struck reeds and the Vivi-Tone Clavier

The six struck-metal and related actions below are playable in Electric Pianos. Electra-Piano and Elepian have production circuit drawings. On the modern forks, the most useful next fixture is a velocity-marked harp send and simultaneous loaded output, followed by a known signal injected at the preamp return where the instrument provides one.

| Target build | Evidence in hand | First decision or missing measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hohner Electra-Piano, schematic 680209 | 72-key engine, three reference forks, 680209 circuit and ten recorded pitches | Measure the fork geometry, winding impedance, group boundaries and pedal-marked direct output |
| Rhodes MK8 S and MK8 FX | 73-key harp, standard board, four optional effects and two production FX recordings | Acquire velocity-matched direct sends, calibrated return sweeps and compressor step responses |
| Vintage Vibe 64 Classic and original active StereoVibe | 64-key harp, passive panel and separately selectable Rev 4.1 active reconstruction | Trace both boards, measure winding impedance and capture a matched dry/stereo recording |

| Vivi-Tone Clavier C 1004 | 73-key C 1004 model, original action inspection, two-ended Loar reed, museum recording and separate restored takes | Measure actual bearings, magnetic circuit and bass-rail split, and resolve the slow modeled bass attack |

Record the specimen, regulation, measurement point and electrical load alongside each new measurement. The original implementation sequence and outstanding specimen tests follow.

## Shared mechanics and bench

1. The Stage 73 voice already maintains modal displacement and velocity through repeated strikes, drives the modes with one finite contact force, and applies progressive damping. Its modal weights, frequencies, tip zones, hammer mass ratio and release curves describe particular Rhodes builds. Prototype a second instrument with a small set of measured modes and its own contact data before moving a reusable solver into shared code. Start with the existing oscillator rotation and event scheduler. [Stage 73 voice](../../stage73/src/tine.rs) · [shared primitives](../../pleasant-dsp/src/instrument.rs)
2. Magnetic instruments can share the calculation of voltage from motion through a field, `e = −N ∇Φ · v`. Each pickup needs the appropriate pole shape, rest position, polarity, lateral sensitivity and coil load. Stage 73 tabulates a chamfered round pole with a fixed radius. The MK8 documents a flat-ended head. OpenWurli's reed and through-slot capacitive pickup form a separate reference for reeds and charge readout. Its 200A field table and polarizing circuit belong to that geometry. [Stage 73 field](../../stage73/src/field.rs) · [OpenWurli reed](../../openwurli-ui/vendor/openwurli-dsp/src/reed.rs) · [OpenWurli pickup](../../openwurli-ui/vendor/openwurli-dsp/src/pickup.rs)
3. Start each instrument with bass, middle and treble keys plus keys either side of a change in construction. Capture several repeats at three measured strike speeds, free decay, early and late key release, pedal catch, pedal release and a restrike into moving metal. Collect source motion where available, open or known-load pickup voltage, then the loaded panel output. Record coil resistance/inductance and cable/interface impedance. Fit resonances and losses to motion or free-decay peaks, pickup geometry to level and harmonic changes, and electronics to electrical excitation. This order prevents a filter from concealing a wrong hammer or pickup.
4. Reserve at least one key per construction zone and an intermediate strike speed for comparison after fitting. Report fundamental pitch, identifiable inharmonic peaks, attack H2/H1 and H3/H1, early and late fundamental decay slopes, release time to −20 dB and inter-strike variation. Use a single comparison gain per recording chain. Mark partials below the measured noise floor as unresolved. Set acceptance bands from repeatability and documented tolerances before fitting. A model that misses the direction of a level, overtone or damping change fails that gate even if its average spectrum agrees. Reuse the Stage 73 offline measurement routines where their windows and peak detection suit the new recording. [Stage 73 measurements](../../stage73/research/measurements.py) · [calibration and holdouts](../../stage73/README.md)

## Hohner Electra-Piano

Target the 72-key Hohner covered by its *General Servicing Instructions*, drawing 680209. A rebound hammer excites a suspended asymmetric fork per key. One prong is sensed inductively. Adjacent coils have opposing connections for interference rejection, and seven series-coil groups have individual balance trims. The right pedal lifts the dampers. The left pedal switches an adjustable electrical attenuation set by P8. The external-amplifier socket, transistor power amplifier and cabinet provide distinct observation points. [Hohner service guide, “Method of Operation,” “Service Guide” and schematic 680209](https://www.clavinet.com/hohner_electrapiano_service_manual.pdf)

1. **Electrical reference.** Trace the seven groups and P1–P9 from the drawing, recording where note ranges and coil impedances require a specimen. Build a netlist through the additional-amplifier socket and the speaker load. Check small-signal gain and phase against the netlist at low, middle and full volume, with the left pedal in both states. Preserve coil winding and magnet signs separately. A useful fixture applies the same external flux to adjacent coils to check hum cancellation while allowing each struck fork to produce its measured polarity.
2. **Three forks.** Measure prong lengths and sections, hammer strike point and dwell, damper position, pickup position and suspended mounting. Derive or identify the coupled normal modes from free decay and the relative motion of the two arms. Drive the measured strike point with a rebounding contact and observe the sensed prong. Check whether beating is present before assigning closely spaced modes. Fit each representative fork to pickup voltage and record the unmeasured parameters with their uncertainty.
3. **Whole piano.** Add keys by coil group and mechanical register. Exercise adjacent notes across every trim boundary, then sustain and release a chord. The right pedal must extend the mechanical ring. Engaging the left pedal during a held note must change electrical level without restarting the note or accelerating the underlying fork decay. Compare the additional-amplifier jack first. Advance the speaker path when there is a matching cabinet measurement or a characterized speaker/load model.

**Three-key implementation.** C3, F4 and G♯5 now render both arm displacements, coil EMF, auxiliary jack and loaded speaker terminals. In three held-out notes from an effects-off hardware performance, the maximum attack H2/H3 error was 1.9 dB. The corresponding early fundamental decay slopes agreed within 0.2 dB/s. A separate 1968 console retains stronger upper partials on C3 than the model’s 80–180 ms ring. The 18-setting ngspice sweep verifies both small-signal taps against drawing 680209 with estimated coil, transformer and device parameters. The remaining gate is an identified specimen with per-register geometry, measured impact velocities, direct-jack recordings and pedal timings. [Bench, evidence and reproduction](../../electra-piano/README.md)

**Native scale.** The live 72-key engine reuses those actions, both pedals and the 680209 output taps. Seven additional recorded pitches check the interpolation and extrapolation. B♭4 attack H2/H3 agreed within 2.5 dB. At F♯6 the model understated H2 by 16.7 dB, and at F6 it lost about 33 dB/s too quickly. The real-time stress report covers 1–72 notes at 44.1/48/96 kHz. [Full-compass evidence and API](../../electra-piano/research/live.md)

## Rhodes MK8

The MK8 S now has its own 73-key tapered-beam harp, flat-pole flux model, direct send and stereo standard preamp. The builder's flat-EQ and Vari-Pan takes and an independent production playing session provide frequency, harmonic and release comparisons. The separate factory FX option now has two production-instrument chorus references, three phaser sweeps and two stereo delay-time measurements. Exact production tip boundaries, coil network, board schematic, calibrated compressor sweeps and isolated direct-send recordings remain open. [FX implementation and measurements](../../rhodes-mk8/research/fx.md) [Implementation, tests and hardware comparisons](../../rhodes-mk8/README.md)

Build the 73-key dry harp first, then the MK8 standard preamp, then the optional FX-equipped signal path. Rhodes specifies steel tines and tonebars, four hammer-tip zones, wool damper felts and flat-ended Alnico pickups. The send provides direct pickup output, while the return permits separate preamp measurements. The standard electronics include drive, envelope control, active three-band EQ with a resonant swept mid and stereo Vari-Pan. The FX option adds VCA compression, phaser, bucket-brigade chorus and delay. [Rhodes MK8 specifications](https://rhodesmusic.com/rhodes-mk8-specifications/) · [hardware guide, connections, effects and voicing](https://rhodesmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/MK8-User-Guide.pdf)

1. Record the serial/build, hammer-zone boundaries, physical tine-to-pole heights and gaps, and damper regulation. Capture each side of every tip transition at the send into a documented load. Fit new fork modes, velocity-to-hammer response and release curves. Use a flat-faced field model and compare harmonic balance and level on a measured gap/height sweep. Preserve the current Stage 73 eras and their vintage calibration while proving this new mechanical profile.
2. With FX bypassed, drive the return with stepped sines and repeatable transients at several levels. Measure drive harmonics, each EQ control's range, envelope attack/recovery and both channels' pan waveforms, phase and depth. Compare the manual's routing and the measured transfer at the output. Identify blocks by measurement when a revision-matched component drawing is unavailable, and label the resulting model accordingly. Gate the standard build on both a held-out dry performance at the send and the corresponding stereo outputs.
3. Stage the FX model separately on an FX-equipped unit. Measure compressor gain reduction and recovery, phaser notch trajectories, chorus modulation, delay repeats and feedback, then two effects in series to establish ordering. Hold back any block whose response cannot be isolated. Stage 73's optocell Peterson panner and Jordan preamp have separately documented circuits and do not establish the MK8's transfer. [Stage 73 era evidence](../../stage73/research/eras.md)

## Vintage Vibe Tine Piano

The original passive 64 Classic now has a playable 64-action model, separate open-circuit pickup, loaded-harp and jack taps, an extended MIDI compass and pickup regulation. Its replacement-scale geometry and spring, damper and recording comparisons are documented, including the remaining 11–14 dB bending-level discrepancy on three check attacks. The three-control network is a testable passive hypothesis. Bench identification still needs a revision-matched board or sweep. [Implemented model, source audit and comparisons](../../vintage-vibe-64/README.md)

The separate active StereoVibe configuration uses the same physical harp with an alternate input, preamp and stereo tremolo path. The service bulletin identifies board revision 4.1 and its modifications. The level, filter and clock measurements and their limits are tracked with that reconstruction. [Active board](../../vintage-vibe-64/src/stereo.rs) · [stereo recording comparison](../../vintage-vibe-64/research/stereovibe.py)

Use the original **64 Classic with the passive panel** as the first reference. Its launch sheet identifies an asymmetrical fork, passive volume and two tone controls, and an optional active stereo preamp. A present-generation Classic 64 is advertised with active electronics and dual-capstan action. Record installed parts and publication generation before assigning features. Vintage Vibe's Vibanet uses a separate string action. [Vintage Vibe launch document, p. 3](https://www.fenderrhodes.com/pdf/64classic.pdf) · [current Classic 64 specification](https://vintagevibepiano.com/electric-piano-models/classic-64/) · [Vibanet and tangent-string plan](tangent-strings.md)

1. Document the 64-key compass by measured pitches, tine lengths and tuning springs, tonebar suspension, hammer tips, strike line, escapement and damper travel on the reference instrument. Inspect pickup head and coil wiring. Fit the modal/contact approach from Stage 73 using the Vintage Vibe measurements. Test held-out notes inside each distinct tip or tine zone, hard restrikes and sustain release.
2. Trace the reference passive panel and measure the pot values, capacitor values and winding load. Compare measured and simulated output for both cable loads and several knob positions. Gate the baseline on its passive jack, with one gain alignment held across a soft-to-hard series. An active StereoVibe unit needs its own preamp board revision and stereo depth/rate measurements. The 2014 service bulletin identifies a particular revision trail. [Maker's service bulletins](https://www.vintagevibe.com/pages/service-bulletins)
3. For a later dual-capstan or Variable Voice Control build, measure hammer throw, key dip and escapement separately. The voice lever moves the pickup rail. Measure relative rail displacement at each end and at center, then predict the harmonic change at several lever positions while keeping mechanical excitation fixed. Require a held-out lever setting to agree before enabling it as a control. The same three-key bench can serve a Deluxe/Marquis follow-up after identifying its hardware. [Dual-capstan action and US 11,410,635](https://www.vintagevibe.com/pages/dual-capstan-action) · [floating pickup rail](https://vintagevibepiano.com/features/variable-voice-control/)

The active option now follows the Rev 4.1 board pictured in the 2014 bulletin with its four serviced resistors. Ginty’s separately identified active 64 Classic produces a 1.836 Hz alternating pan. The later comparison window gives a normalized envelope discrepancy of 0.063. The input loading and EQ values are estimates pending a component trace. [StereoVibe research and independent checks](../../vintage-vibe-64/research/stereovibe.md)

## Columbia Elepian

The **EP-61C** now has a playable weighted-reed action and the passive circuit shown in a technician’s original manual. The service film identifies separate pickup clearance and lateral alignment screws. Twelve independent circuit sweeps match within 0.000004 dB. Two physical performances give median harmonic differences of 2.95 dB at the raw harp and 5.84 dB at the shop instrument’s output. [Model and evidence](../../pianet-n/research/elepian.md) · [Original EP-61C drawings at 3:40 and 3:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kv1VYiWfWxU)

The portable **EP-61C** is the target. A technician-serviced specimen has hammer-struck metal plates, volume and tone, a single output and a damper pedal. Its highest C was reported faulty, so omit that particular note from calibration. The period catalogue distinguishes the passive EP-61C and active EP-7C. Nippon Columbia's patent US 4,175,461 supplies a testable magnetic pickup candidate, including measured spectra as vibrating amplitude and pole coverage change. Its assignment establishes authorship. Production fitment still needs direct inspection. [Inspected EP-61C and photos](https://organ69.net/ep010.shtml) · [October 1981 Columbia catalogue](https://guitar-catalog.com/keyboard-and-effector/columbia/1981/en_index.html) · [Nippon Columbia patent, figs. 11–18](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4175461)

1. Photograph an identified EP-61C generator from the side and above. Document reed stock, clamp, tip mass if present, strike orientation, contact and damper lift. Identify coil, magnet, pole extent, offsets, wiring and passive jack by inspection and continuity. A passive jack alone does not determine pole shape. Use the patent's variable-length pole simulation as a bench study. Match the patent's own reference conditions before deciding whether the field model belongs in the EP-61C.
2. Model struck bending modes from EP-61C measurements. Use motion at the pickup to drive the observed field law, then the actual volume/tone network. Capture dry notes at the extremes of pickup position and both panel controls. The fixture must reproduce the change in harmonic balance with reed excursion on a held-out note and the measured jack impedance versus frequency. Keep the instrument at prototype status until the generator geometry and panel response both agree.
3. **Historical variant boundary.** The US Electrokey/Maestro spinet built by Nippon Columbia has front-struck vertical reeds and capacitive detection. The USITC illustrates that pickup in exhibit A, and the court identifies its manufacturer. If that specific instrument becomes the target, derive its electrode overlap and bias/loading network separately using the documented geometry and an identified unit. Compare current generated by known reed motion to the historical circuit before integrating. The Japanese portable and American spinet are separate profiles. [USITC publication 721, pp. A-2 and A-20](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/337/pub721.pdf) · [court's instrument identification](https://openjurist.org/380/fsupp/576/wurlitzer-co-v-electrokey-inc)

## Valente Electric Piano

The production 61-key model now uses a weighted driver and moving counter-hammer, a shouldered steel reed derived from the maker’s 1:25 finite-element rendering, per-note magnetic pickup, sympathetic harp and passive volume/brilliance. It includes a damper pedal, pickup-distance adjustment and full MIDI extension. Fifteen Fonseca and five Doykin recorded pitches yielded 4.05 and 14.81 dB median H2–H6 discrepancies. Replayable model taps, listening comparisons and twelve independent electrical sweeps are documented. [Implementation, hardware evidence and comparisons](../../pianet-n/research/valente.md)

The action geometry, pole clearances, winding grouping, board trace, case vibration and absolute gain still need physical measurement on an identified production piano. A velocity-marked clean jack recording with two measured loads and damper times would distinguish these estimates. ## Vivi-Tone Clavier

The C 1004 reconstruction uses a pivoting picker that lifts a flat steel through-bar, two spaced bearings, a rear magnetic armature and two independent damper lifts. The removed picker, wooden mounting and felt mechanism are visible in the museum technician’s demonstration at 13:08–14:21. Loar’s drawings identify the two vibrating ends, jack escape and rearming after one-third key return. The plugin includes the original nameboard control and three pedals, pickup-gap adjustment and full MIDI extension. [Action and circuit patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1992317A/en) · [examination of C 1004](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Edomefgocsg&t=788s) · [implementation and source audit](../../pianet-n/research/vivi-tone.md)

The surviving 73-key museum instrument had a silent lower third when recorded. Fourteen pitches from its surviving register and 27 separately recorded, restored pitches yielded median H2 discrepancies of 5.63 and 2.55 dB. The modeled C1 takes 59 ms to rise against 26 ms on the restored recording. The lower circuit, component values, bass damper boundary and absolute gain remain reconstructions. [Catalog entry](https://emuseum.nmmusd.org/objects/16691/vivitone-clavier) · [technician's separate recording and licensing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cEG7hD28dW4)
