# Electric Pianos model similarity audit

## Ranked findings

The release VST3 produced **6,834 isolated strikes and 105 model/setting phrase renders** across **35 configurations** representing 33 playable selections or eras and two isolated Duo branches. The audit contains **144 distinct pairs** and **330 pair/setting rows**. Smaller screening scores indicate closer measured responses. The plugin findings below have high confidence under the tested settings. The hardware conclusions distinguish direct evidence from inference.

1. **Early and Mid Mark I · nearly identical stock, exact through Peterson.** Stock score **0.009**, with a **0.12 dB** median H2–H6 difference. The Peterson route at zero intensity matches **57/57 strikes and the full phrase** sample for sample. At minimum passive tone the harmonic difference widens to **8.92 dB** and Mid is **6.72 dB** louder in the note body. The schematics are in use. The common action and bypass of both era-specific passive panels explain the collapse. High confidence in the modeled cause and panel distinction. The audible contribution of the Mid hybrid hammer remains unmeasured. [Stock A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/01-stock.wav) · [Passive tone A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/16-stage-tone-min.wav) · [Era evidence](../../stage73/research/eras.md)
2. **Vintage Vibe 64 and StereoVibe · very close dry, a documented shared harp.** Stock score **0.011**, with **0.15 dB** median harmonic difference and **0.14 dB** release difference. With tremolo enabled the width difference rises from zero to **0.458** on the audit's 0–1 scale. The FET input also changes the release measurement substantially (**19.19 dB**). The shared harp matches the product documentation. The dry voicing and FET loading remain provisional. An independent active Classic recording supports the pan shape, with a third modulation harmonic of −17.53 dBc recorded and −17.04 dBc modeled. [Stock A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/02-stock.wav) · [FET A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/15-stereovibe-fet.wav) · [Stereo tremolo A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/24-stereovibe-tremolo.wav) · [Service bulletin and recording comparison](../../vintage-vibe-64/research/stereovibe.md)
3. **Early and Mid Mark I against Late Mark I · close in the stock jack, separate at the harp.** The two scores are **0.048 and 0.055**. The stock panel and gain trims leave similar envelopes even though the Late harp has its own measured per-key pickup setup and velocity anchors. The independent source taps differ by up to **26.8% RMS** on the three probe keys. The passive tone sweep separates Early and Late further. High confidence in a live mechanical difference. Its size depends on specimen voicing and velocity. [Early/Late A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/03-stock.wav) · [Harp measurements](#what-the-source-taps-isolate)
4. **Original CP-70 and CP-70B · close with a concrete circuit substitution.** Stock score **0.049**. Median harmonic difference is **1.03 dB** and release differs by **0.06 dB**. The CP-70 pair uses distinct harnesses and the same CP-70B panel, optocoupler path and working course parameters. Yamaha's parts list identifies distinct TR boards and wound-string parts. That makes original **NA500150** the clearest circuit gap to close. High confidence in the substitution, limited confidence about how much the original hardware would separate. CP-70B versus CP-80 scores **0.313** and differs by **3.92 dB** across H2–H6. [Yamaha parts list](https://manuals.fdiskc.com/tree/Yamaha/Yamaha%20Electric%20Grand%20Parts%20List%20for%20CP-70%20CP-70B%20CP-80.pdf) · [Original/B A/B](../../target/model-similarity/listening/04-stock.wav)
5. **Claviset 200 and Selmer Pianotron · close and underconstrained.** Stock score **0.060**, with **0.30 dB** median harmonic difference, **0.55 dB** release difference and **3.81 dB** stock note-body level difference. The paired register and vibrato settings only widen the score to **0.067** and **0.093**. Both use an estimated common blade and pole scale. Some similarity may be genuine given the shared product lineage, but the 200 pickup revision is unresolved. A service catalogue lists a 200/300 drawing without a publicly linked scan. The independent Pianotron harmonic comparison had a median **12.1 dB** H2 error over 13 notes. High confidence in overlap in the plugin, low confidence that the common pickup accurately represents both specimens. 6. **Pianet C, N and L · a common action with distinct circuit behavior.** C/N is the closest Hohner menu pair at **0.082**, with **0.53 dB** release difference. L/N has only **0.20 dB** median harmonic difference at the external jack and **6.43 dB** difference at key-up. C/L differs by **0.15 dB** in stock note-body level. At half knee L is **38.47 dB** quieter, consistent with the prior 440 Hz board comparison at **38.8 dB**. The local output selects headphones on C and speakers on L. Wrapper trims compensate their raw terminal voltage. Original circuit details clearly reach the audio. The specimen-dependent adhesive pull-off and release remain weaker evidence. 7. **D6, E7 and the Duo's Clavinet · similar action, audible electronics.** D6/E7 scores **0.095** stock and **0.131** on the paired pickup. E7 is **2.03 dB** louder at stock volume and **11.79 dB** quieter at half travel. E7 and the Duo branch score **0.122** with a **1.57 dB** harmonic difference. They share action ancestry and related transistor preamps, and the Duo's schematic and split routing make it distinct at the full-instrument level. D6/E7 directly implement the circuit revisions. The common plectrum is visible in the manual. The early RF detector and N biased electrode feed distinct circuits. The remaining discrepancy is absolute N output against factory tests and the unmeasured reed geometry, so there is a stronger case for calibrating those stages than for differentiating the common action. ### Other families

- **Duo Pianet and Pianet T** are neighbors at **0.219**. They share a suction-pad and magnetic-reed architecture, with **1.05 dB** median harmonic difference and **5.70 dB** difference at key-up. The T uses a passive transformer bank and the Duo reed branch uses a transistor stage before its separate recording tap. In the full Duo, the string branch, split and registration provide further separation. [Duo circuit and specimen recordings](../../hohner-duo/README.md)
- **MK8 and Vintage Vibe versus Stage 73** have upstream differences. The closest new tine model to a vintage Stage era is **MK8 / Mark V**, at **0.873**, including **5.17 dB** harmonic and **16.17 dB** release differences. Different action and pickup behavior is visible before the electronics. Their panel reference gaps still matter for fidelity. [Modern tine source-tap checks](#what-the-source-taps-isolate)
- **Struck strings beyond Yamaha** have distinct readouts. The closest pair outside the Yamaha revisions is Baldwin/Storytone at **0.627**, with **8.49 dB** harmonic difference. Baldwin/Roadmaster scores **0.710** with **10.23 dB** release difference. Baldwin uses a pressure rail, Storytone uses magnetic plates and a dual-primary input, and Roadmaster uses magnetic strips and a coupled board. The similar aggregate envelopes do not establish a shared pickup or electronics path. [Upright signal paths](#struck-strings)
- **Electric Uprights** presents Storytone and Vivi-Tone in the same menu, but their mechanisms and measurements are far apart. They score **3.726**, with **37.13 dB** median harmonic difference. **Other metal resonators** are also more spread out. Electra/Valente is the closest at **0.550**, with **5.17 dB** harmonic difference, despite one using coupled fork arms and the other a counter-hammer and reed. The three header defaults score **1.036–1.413** against each other. [Metal resonator paths](#other-metal-resonators)
- **Vibanet** remains distinct from its Clavinet relatives at stock and on the bridge and phase pairs. D6-derived dimensions still limit the action model. Its gel damping and active network warrant checking against the existing measured release and pickup differences. [Vibanet hardware comparisons](../../clavinet-d6/research/vibanet/README.md)

## Follow-up priorities

1. **Trace original Yamaha CP-70 NA500150.** Seek a legible factory drawing or a documented pre-B board with matching pickups. Walk the harness into the first stage, then measure loaded gain, EQ and optical tremolo. The parts list proves the board revision and the live engine currently substitutes the B's panel.
2. **Get the Claviset 200/300 sheets and identify the target 200's pickup revision.** Check the actual register network and source loading, then trace Selmer serial 877 or an equivalent 8701 specimen. Identifying an RF or biased pickup versus a coil would change the transducer model. A sweep of each register, one mid-key spectrum and two held-out registers would constrain the reconstruction. [Original manufacturer archive](https://www.vermona.com/en/support/vermona-heritage/) · [Selmer specimen 877](https://www.spheremusic.com/Bargaindtl.asp?Item=12034)
3. **Measure the Early/Mid mechanical difference.** Use matching pickup height and escapement on dated square-tip and hybrid-shank harps. Record several strikes per pitch with measured velocity and capture at the harp and passive jack. This isolates how much independent era voicing the Peterson model should retain.
4. **Check the Hohner approximations with targeted bench inputs.** For D6/E7, use a loaded input/THD sweep at the same physical pickup amplitudes before adding dynamic nonlinear DSP. For C/L/N, record pad pull-off and release at controlled speeds on identified action and board revisions. For Cembalet N, compare loaded voltage at all three service taps and verify the pickup bias and gap before adding gain.
5. **Confirm the modern board loads.** Use the StereoVibe Rev 4.1 bulletin and a specimen to measure FET/parallel input behavior, release and optical loading. Obtain a production MK8 send/return sweep to replace the standard-board winding, diode and input estimates. The available stereo recordings already constrain a large part of the modulation behavior.

## What the source taps isolate

- **Early and Mid Mark I.** At MIDI 40, 60 and 84 and velocity 80, the matched 48 kHz harp WAVs are sample-identical. Late Mark I differs by 17.5–26.8% RMS relative to the Early harp on those same strikes. These comparisons place the Early/Mid difference in their passive panels and provide a separate check that the raw tap can detect a real voicing change.
- **D6 and E7.** The 44.1 kHz pilot renders are sample-identical at both pickups for MIDI 46, 64 and 78. Both corresponding solo jacks differ. With one input voltage, the circuit predicts 5.8 dB less gain in E7 at 100 Hz and 10.6 dB less at 1.6 kHz. The stock wrapper compensates part of the gain, and the E7 slider taper makes the jacks separate further at half travel. Both circuits use the Hohner drawings. The priority omissions are the live small-signal approximation and E7-specific regulation measurements. [Independent circuit sweep and nonlinear simulation](../../clavinet-d6/research/e7/README.md)
- **Stage 73, Vintage Vibe, MK8.** The existing matched C4 source-tap recordings at velocity 80 put attack energy above 1.8 kHz at −41.4 dB of the total for Stage, −17.3 dB for the passive Vintage Vibe source and −18.9 dB at the MK8 send. The Vintage Vibe jack moves its result by 0.7 dB. The harp and send comparisons support a substantial difference upstream of the panels. The Vintage Vibe replacement-length chart is common to both older tines, with the C4 smooth-length substitution changing the attack result by 0.09 dB. ## Reproduce the measurements

From the workspace root, with Pedalboard, Mido and NumPy installed in the same Python environment:

```sh
cargo run -p openwurli-ui-xtask --release -- bundle openwurli-ui --release
python3 openwurli-ui/research/model_similarity.py \
  'target/bundled/Electric Pianos.vst3' target/model-similarity
```

The renderer uses 48 kHz and 128-sample blocks, a 40 ms lead-in, a 750 ms held note and a 500 ms release. Velocity is 40, 80 or 115. It generates eight evenly spaced pitches across each common native compass and adds documented adjacent boundary keys. The phrase adds repeated notes, chords and CC64 down/up. The Duo's stock jack follows its stock A/B footswitch behavior. Its separate recording outputs are analyzed as individual mono branches. This preserves Duo layer behavior and avoids counting recording-channel placement as a difference against mono instruments.

The score is an equally weighted mean of scaled differences in attack/body RMS, attack rise time, H2–H6, held decay, key-up loss, body gain from velocity 40 to 115, stereo width and the shared phrase envelope. The scales are 3 dB, 12 ms, 3 dB, 4 dB, 5 dB, 4 dB, 0.25 and 4 dB respectively. The measurements use the median and give each pair equal treatment within the note grid. The score screens for candidates and has no calibrated audibility threshold. The CSV retains absolute levels and exact sample hashes independently. Attack rise uses the first 1 ms window to reach 90% of the maximum attack envelope. Harmonics under −60 dBc are floored. Audition WAVs alternate A and B at each pitch and then on the phrase, with one RMS-matching gain per take and a shared export gain.

The generated files are `comparisons.csv`, `strikes.csv`, `provenance.json`, and the WAVs and take order in `listening/`. Unavailable harmonics above Nyquist are blank in the per-strike CSV. The source-tap comparisons are separate engine probes with their own sample rates. To reproduce the fresh taps, use `cargo run -p stage73 --release --example probe -- note FILE 48000 MIDI 0.62992125984 0.75 0 0 ERA harp` for `early_i`, `mid_i` and `late_i`, and `cargo run -p clavinet-d6 --release --bin clavinet-d6 -- DIR 80 .75 da-adds 15 MODEL` for `d6` and `e7`. The latter writes both raw pickups and the solo jack outputs for the three probe keys.

Pedalboard renders and resets every phrase. Its normal per-take reset can rebuild all of the plugin's engines. The accelerated run uses the same VST3 executable through an offline host that initializes the component once and calls VST3 `setProcessing` off and on for each isolated strike. It verifies MIDI 64 at velocity 80 against a fresh Pedalboard render in every model/setting. Models that exceed the agreement tolerance use Pedalboard for their strike grid as well. The provenance records the maximum sample delta and every fallback. The direct host sends the same MIDI events with 128-frame blocks. Its fresh reset reaches the same plugin code that the VST3 wrapper uses to clear DSP buffers. [Pedalboard host reset behavior](https://github.com/spotify/pedalboard/blob/master/pedalboard/ExternalPlugin.h) · [Local VST3 reset path](../../Damian%20Channel%20Strip/vendor/nih-plug/src/wrapper/vst3/wrapper.rs)

On the macOS checkout used for this audit, the optional helper builds against the workspace's compiled VST3 bindings and its loader crate. From the workspace root after building the bundle:

```sh
deps=target/release/deps
vst3_bindings="$(find "$deps" -name 'libvst3_sys-*.rlib' -print -quit)"
dynamic_loader="$(find "$deps" -name 'liblibloading-*.rlib' -print -quit)"
mkdir -p target/model-similarity
rustc --edition=2021 --crate-type cdylib -O openwurli-ui/research/model_similarity_vst3.rs \
  -L "dependency=$deps" --extern "vst3_sys=$vst3_bindings" --extern "libloading=$dynamic_loader" \
  -o target/model-similarity/libmodel_similarity_vst3.dylib
python3 openwurli-ui/research/model_similarity.py \
  'target/bundled/Electric Pianos.vst3' target/model-similarity \
  --native-vst3 target/model-similarity/libmodel_similarity_vst3.dylib
```

Hardware evidence here comes from the referenced service drawings, bench traces, and previously recorded specimens. Playback velocity, regulation, cabinet and microphone differences limit comparisons to the recorded conditions. The generated A/Bs allow level-matched listening, while the ranked findings use the measured features and signal paths. A controlled listener panel and new serial-matched hardware sessions remain open.
