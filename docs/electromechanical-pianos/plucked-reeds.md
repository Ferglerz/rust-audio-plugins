# Plucked-reed pianos

Start with a Pianet N whose amplifier board can be matched to a Hohner drawing. Its action, electrode, modulation and output levels each have an independent source. The C and L can then reuse the early pad model. The T introduces a suction pad and magnetic pickup, and unlocks the Pianet side of the Duo. The Cembalet has particularly good documentation for an independent plectrum and damper. Reserve the Claviset and Pianotron for a specimen-led pass.

## Shared mechanical work

1. **Characterize one bass, middle and treble key before filling the keyboard.** Record reed length from clamp to tip, width and thickness along its length, pad or plucker contact position, sense position, rest offset and excursion in millimetres. Photograph the reed clamp, excitation and pickup together, with a scale. Take simultaneous key-travel and isolated line-output traces for slow and fast depression, a held note, release and an immediate repeat. Identify the instrument, restoration parts, output load, swell position and modulation setting with each take.
2. **Build a single-key mechanical probe.** Fit a clamped beam to observed mode frequencies and free-decay slopes. Allow section changes where the reed has been profiled or filed. Verify overtones against displacement or velocity measured before the pickup where available. Electrical harmonics at multiples of the fundamental can come from the pickup even when no mechanical mode exists at those frequencies. Retain enough mechanical modes for convergence in the audible band.
3. **Connect the key to the beam at the actual contact patch.** Use modal coordinates for displacement and velocity and preserve both at separation and recontact. For early sticky pads, start with a compliant pad resting on the reed and lifting it until restoring force exceeds adhesion. For suction pads, fit a separate pull-off curve against preload, area and key speed. For a plectrum or cam, release follows clearance of an edge. In every case apply damping at the physical point of return. Use the measured attack-speed dependence to map MIDI velocity to a family of key trajectories. These excitation laws are modeling proposals. Zacharias supplies a mechanical basis for the early adhesive release and the adjustable electrode. [Hohner patent US3423514A, Figs. 1–4 and claims 26–30](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3423514A/en)
4. **Keep the pickup and load outside the mechanical voice.** For a DC-biased capacitive plate, derive the capacitance curve from the target electrode geometry, sum moving capacitances on their actual electrical nodes, and solve charge conservation through the drawn bias and input impedances. For a magnetic bank, derive the signed flux linkage at each coil from reed motion, sum induced voltages according to winding connections and apply the bank load once. The early RF Cembalet needs the separate carrier/detector model below. Compare quiet chords with sums of individual keys to locate any common-stage nonlinearity.

The existing oscillator rotation and sample-accurate event scheduler can be used directly. OpenWurli shows how to seed signed modal states and derive a nonlinear pickup from geometry. Its seven-mode tables, hammer-contact response, Wurlitzer comb geometry, polarization and preamp are specific to that instrument. Stage 73's field lookup demonstrates how to precompute a flux gradient, but its round, chamfered pole and tine require a different field than a magnetized blade above a pole shoe. Use the Electric Grand 80 probe as a pattern for running deterministic input through a complete instrument. ## Hohner Pianet N

The [playable N and three-key reference](../../pianet-n/README.md) cover F1–F6, a working swell and 6 Hz optical modulation. The isolated A2, E4 and A5 runs trace pad lift, adhesive separation, damping and partial reseating. The early circuit follows Hohner 30 002 167 D, including separate 100/300/600 mV taps. A middle triad gives peak jack ratios of 1:2.96:5.98 into 10 MΩ. The 600 mV outlet reaches 397 mV in the separate 7 kΩ receiver simulation. The remaining measurements include matched dry hardware strikes, transformer impedance, lamp and ORP60 dynamics and settled pad travel on an identified specimen. [Factory circuit](https://www.clavinet.com/Pianet_N_schematic.pdf) · [Hohner triad and output data](https://www.manualslib.de/manual/1459627/Hohner-Pianet-N.html?page=5)

**First build.** Choose the board using a photograph of the fitted components and output sockets. Hohner published two N drawings. The documented early circuit couples a bank of biased reed electrodes to a transistor preamp. A lamp and photoresistor vary gain at roughly 6 Hz. The pad preloads the reed at rest and damps it again on key return. A restored N in the Powerhouse collection documents its replacement pads, a detail that materially affects attack calibration. [N service instructions, function and electrode regulation](https://www.manualslib.de/manual/1459627/Hohner-Pianet-N.html?page=6) · [N schematic](https://www.clavinet.com/Pianet_N_schematic.pdf) · [N version II schematic](https://www.clavinet.com/Pianet_N_Version2_schematic.pdf) · [Powerhouse specimen record](https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/612341)

1. Fit the early pad probe to repeated takes from the identified N, including incompletely released repeats. Measure deflection just before detachment, release time after key-up and the limited change of attack with key speed. Disable sustain-pedal hold in the period action. Verify that key-up damps the reed even if CC64 is held. Document the fitted pad material.
2. Reconstruct the electrode bias, coupling, transistor stages, swell and output taps from the matching sheet. Use the actual node voltages and impedances when deriving a lower-cost audio equivalent. Check input-current-to-output gain and phase against an offline circuit reference. Add lamp/LDR dynamics once the dry transfer is correct.
3. Run Hohner's own test on the applicable revision. With the swell open, a mid-keyboard triad should produce approximately 100, 300 and 600 mV on the three specified outlets. Record the exact triad, port load, measurement convention and key speed, and check the **ratios** as well as the order of magnitude. Measure modulation depth and rate on a held note. For the second N revision, compare its board, port levels and optical/electronic modulation path against its own sheet before promoting it as another version. [Hohner output test and connection diagram](https://www.manualslib.de/manual/1459627/Hohner-Pianet-N.html?page=3)

The drawings support circuit work now. Target-revision electrode clearances, pad pull-off data and matched dry takes are the remaining voicing dependencies.

## Hohner Pianet C

The board dated 28 February 1963 now drives a separate external amplifier socket and AC125/AC126 headphone branch. Knee regulation, optical sidebands and six decaying components in an owner recording have been checked. [Implementation and evidence](../../pianet-n/research/pianet-cl.md)

**Early-pad derivative.** The C archive includes the original manual, knee swell and original plucker photos. Use the model-C drawing as the circuit bill of materials. Identify the fitted board and available switches on the reference unit. The separate CH amplifier is a distinct accessory. [Hohner C schematic](https://www.clavinet.com/Pianet_C_Schematic.pdf) · [C manual and original parts photographs](https://hohner-pianet.com/pianet_C.html) · [Hohner drawing index including CH amp](https://www.clavinet.com/schematics.php)

1. From the identified C, seed the lever, pad and reed geometry using the N probe. Recheck pad placement, stiffness, electrode alignment and the scale at both ends of the keyboard. Fit any differences from C observations.
2. Trace the capacitive bank, preamp, swell and output from the C drawing and verify the knee-lever taper on the actual unit. Include a modulation branch only for an identified configuration with the corresponding circuit.
3. At a fixed swell, compare measured and rendered low, middle and high note attacks, free decay and abrupt key-up. Then take the same key at three swell positions. Approve the C variant when these ratios and the circuit sweep agree independently. C-specific measurements are the prerequisite to carrying a C label.

## Hohner Pianet L

The E71 mains variant now drives the external-amplifier socket and the two series 5 Ω speakers separately. Nine decaying components from an owner’s built-in-speaker performance give a median 2.54 dB/s difference. The external jack and speaker-terminal SPICE checks agree within 0.0048 dB. [Implementation and evidence](../../pianet-n/research/pianet-cl.md)

**Amplified early-pad derivative.** The L drawing includes a transistor amplifier, a reed/electrode section, speaker connections and a mechanical view. The L manual and original pad photographs document the knee swell. Start with the mains L represented by the drawing. [Hohner L circuit and Fig. 6](https://www.clavinet.com/pianet_l_schematic.pdf) · [L/LB manual, knee lever and original pad](https://hohner-pianet.com/pianet_L_LB.html)

1. Measure the L's reed and pad action at the same anchor keys as the N. Fit the passive mechanical probe first, then derive the electrode source and loading from the L. Record the keyboard's electrical output with the swell locked at a repeatable position.
2. Solve the L preamp, power stage and connected speaker load. Capture the direct electrical path and a fixed-position microphone response from the built-in speakers separately. Derive a cabinet/speaker transfer only after identifying the electrical signal that drives it.
3. Validate gain and headroom with a single key and a chord, then compare the two output paths on the same performance. The direct-output model can be verified independently while speaker/cabinet sensitivity and response await L measurements. The LB battery variant would require its own circuit evidence.

## Hohner Pianet T

The [60-key passive T](../../pianet-n/research/pianet-t.md) now drives a series pickup bank and output transformer from a sealed, leaking suction cup and separate reed at each key. On eight reserved P-layer pitches from Sullivan’s dry T, median residuals are 1.69 cents in pitch, 1.50 dB/s in fundamental decay and 2.51 dB in second-harmonic level. A2’s release burst is 23 ms shorter than the hardware recording. The probe includes the 10 and 100 kΩ load cases and a repeat test exposing cup pressure and reed motion. [Hohner’s T manual](https://www.clavinet.com/pianet_t_manual.pdf) · [Sullivan’s direct-jack method](https://www.sullivang.net/hohner-pianet-t-electric-piano-instrument-for-gigasamplergigastudio)

**Build after the first pad probe, or in parallel with the early circuit work.** Hohner documents a passive keyboard with soft suction pads, weakly magnetized reeds, a series pickup bank with alternating coil connections and an output transformer. Key release immediately damps the reed. Hohner specifies an amplifier input impedance between 10 and 100 kΩ, giving two reproducible electrical load cases. [T service manual](https://www.clavinet.com/pianet_t_manual.pdf) · [Factory manual archive](https://archive.org/details/sm_Hohner_Pianet_T_Service_manual/page/n1/mode/2up)

1. Fit T-specific reed sections and damping. Measure the suction pad's footprint, pull-off displacement and speed dependence on the selected reed surface. Check fast note repeats where the pad has had little time to seat. Compare the controlled slow and fast attacks with the early N plots to expose an accidental carry-over of the N pad law.
2. Measure coil orientation, pole-shoe gap, flux gradient along reed travel, series impedance and transformer ratio over frequency. Convert motion to induced voltage and retain wiring polarity under simultaneous notes. Verify measured output into 10 and 100 kΩ and a known high-impedance DI. Absolute coil impedance and gap remain acquisition items.
3. Use Greg Sullivan's direct-jack recordings for an independent check of attack, steady spectral balance and key return. He reports two playing levels and a single release level. Exclude files marked “plucked” from action validation because he excited those reeds with a toothpick. Use original recorded pitches and bypass sampler velocity switches and envelopes during analysis. Save dry reference metrics separately from synthesized audio. [Owner's recording and excitation notes](https://www.sullivang.net/hohner-pianet-t-electric-piano-instrument-for-gigasamplergigastudio)

The Duo can consume the calibrated suction action and magnetized-reed model. Its service manual shows a distinct onboard electrical system. Validate the Duo's register wiring, split and mix using its own diagram. [Duo manual, Pianet mechanism and wiring](https://civade.com/AudioPro/Hohner/Hohner_Pianet-Clavinet-Duo_Service_Manual.pdf)

## Hohner Cembalet

The early tube and 650720 N engines now implement the complete 61-key compass, their respective receiver circuits, and physical plectrum/damper return. The RF and SPICE comparisons pass. The modeled N output into the three service loads is low and the publisher demo decays faster than the modeled G♯2. Serial-identified dry reference and measured component values remain open. [Implementation, source ledger and reproducible results](../../pianet-n/research/cembalet.md)


**Choose the revision before implementing the electrode.** Hohner's early tube manual supplies a full circuit, parts list, adjustment instructions and drawings. A key lever moves an adjustable plectrum up against the reed until the tip clears. On key return a sprung damper lands while the plectrum slips underneath. The manual attributes overall loudness control to the knee swell and describes essentially fixed pluck strength. The later Cembalet N has a distinct transistor circuit with lamp modulation. [Early Hohner manual, action Fig. 1, circuit and regulation](https://civade.com/AudioPro/Hohner/Hohner_Cembalet_Service_Manual.pdf) · [Cembalet N circuit, Fig. 6](https://civade.com/AudioPro/Hohner/Hohner_Cembalet_N_Schematic.pdf)

1. Begin with the early manual's revision. Measure plectrum overlap, compliance, pivot geometry and damper travel. Model contact until geometric clearance and damp the return stroke before the plectrum resets. At nominal regulation, release should occur around the middle to lower third of the downstroke as described in the manual. Compare a silent partial depression, slow and fast full strokes, repeated notes and key-up reset against action video and DI.
2. The early circuit sums the side electrodes into a tuned receiving network driven near 1.75 MHz. Reed motion detunes that network. The detector recovers an audio envelope. Compute the tuned response versus total capacitance offline, with its actual bias point on the resonance flank. Use a baseband equivalent at audio rate and compare it against an offline carrier-and-detector simulation for single keys and chords. Check knee-swell gain and modulation sidebands separately. Hohner's regulation check uses a triad and then a broad chord to expose detector distortion. [Hohner manual, electrical operation and distortion adjustment](https://civade.com/AudioPro/Hohner/Hohner_Cembalet_Service_Manual.pdf)
3. For an N, measure plectrum and electrode position on a tagged specimen, reconstruct the DC-biased electrode input and optical modulation from its sheet and compare nodes with the early Pianet N drawing. Share genuinely matching amplifier sections. Keep the Cembalet's own geometry and independent damper. Gate both revisions on first-cycle waveform, decay, quiet reset, individual note balance and chord intermodulation. The early circuit is ready for a prototype. Plectrum dimensions, detector tuning under real bank capacitance and reference dry takes still need acquisition. [Cembalet N original instructions and board images](https://hohner-cembalet.com/Cembalet_N.html) · [Cembalet N circuit](https://www.clavinet.com/Cembalet_N_schematic.pdf)

## Weltmeister Claviset and Selmer Pianotron

The magnetic Claviset 200 and the Selmer no. 8701 now have playable cam actions and separate panels. On 13 identified Selmer notes and 11 identified Claviset note/register combinations the median absolute H2–H6 differences from the owners’ instruments are 4.53 and 6.48 dB. The Claviset’s measured modulation rates are 3.71, 6.24 and 8.86 Hz. Serial-matched original circuits, reed/pole dimensions and controlled key-up recordings are still needed. [Implementation and recording audit](../../pianet-n/research/claviset.md)

The Deutsches Museum's Claviset 200 has plucked metal reeds, nine timbre switches and three modulation settings. The hardware preparations documented by Spectrasonics describe a rotating rubber disc with a projecting lip. A first-hand account of a 200 describes capacitive sensing, while Spectrasonics describes magnetic pickups on its sampled instrument. The museum description identifies electrical sensing without naming the type. A service archive lists a combined 200/300 diagram. Its circuit sheet has yet to be verified for either specimen. [Deutsches Museum object](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/%E2%80%9Cweltmeister%E2%80%9C-claviset-200-klingenthaler-harmonikawerke-klingenthal/JgGfrxxBEqUYiQ?hl=en) · [Spectrasonics hardware notes, p. 136](https://www.midimanuals.com/manuals/spectrasonics/keyscape/reference_guide_v1_1/8190_14901_en_1516660869.pdf) · [Czechkeys 200 inspection](https://czechkeys.blogspot.com/2010/05/weltmeister-claviset-200-ukazka.html) · [200/300 schematic catalogue entry](https://www.manuals.altervista.org/Other-Service-Manuals-Schematic-Diagrams.html)

1. **Claviset 200.** Identify a serial, panel, pickup construction and pedal mechanism. Trace the pickup leads to the first amplifier stage and distinguish a coil/transformer from an electrode/bias network. Film one complete cam rotation or reset cycle. Measure the lip radius and compliance, cam angle at escape, reed dimensions and damper contact. The first implementation fixture should predict release and rearm angles from the cam profile. Map tone tabs to measured electrical filter responses and modulation settings to measured rate and depth. Verify separate physical sustain and volume on a 200 so equipped using key-up with sustain on, pedal-up during ringing, and a held chord through each tab. [200 owner demonstration, mechanics at 3:29 and circuitry at 10:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ncOPN2Nn2w) · [Owner's two-pedal 200 observations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HM5sg6Nu_Yk)
2. **UK Selmer Pianotron.** Target the portable 1960s Selmer. A technician's documented unit, serial 877, is identified as a Claviset rebadge and has magnetic pickups, transformer, battery preamp and knee-volume lever. An independent owner documents the cam pluck. Establish the action, keyboard range, controls and actual circuit of the chosen Selmer against photos and the service drawing before assigning it a 200 or 300 profile. A restricted panel and knee swell warrant their own control map. [Selmer period advertisement and instrument photos](https://www.vintagehofner.co.uk/selmer/misc/pianotron.html) · [Serial 877 technician description](https://www.spheremusic.com/Bargaindtl.asp?Item=12034) · [Pianotron cam action](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E5nMy8r2vVM)
3. On the identified magnetic Selmer, fit coil flux and transformer loading from motion and electrical measurements, followed by its preamp and swell. Compare the same cam release at two knee settings and check note-off with the fitted damper. Only reuse the 200/300 cam and reed scale once matching dimensions or held-out captures support it. A serial-matched schematic or a documented trace plus dry key-down/up and switch captures promotes each instrument separately.

## Acceptance for a named instrument

Reserve at least one key per register, one key-speed condition and one chord for validation. Use the same windows, gain reference and estimator for the hardware and rendered captures. Report first-cycle polarity against a known recording polarity, time to release and damper contact, modal frequencies, early and late decay rates, pickup-generated harmonic levels, and measured output under a named load. Derive acceptance bands from repeated captures and the precision of the fixture before tuning the model. Compare reference sweeps with the traced circuit across the useful passband and repeat the deterministic checks at different sample rates and block sizes. Record any parameter that remains a bounded estimate and the capture that would settle it. A mechanism prototype can proceed while those measurements are pending. A named instrument requires the corresponding held-out evidence.
