# Tangent strings — Hohner Clavinet and Vintage Vibe Vibanet

The **Clavinet D6** provides the reference action. Its inventor describes the contact mechanism, Hohner publishes the circuit, and two research groups give us both a numerical reference and observations from real hardware. The **E7** uses the two pickup signals with its own wiring and preamp. The **Clavinet/Pianet Duo** couples the tangent to a second, pad-plucked reed action. The **Vibanet** uses a gel-loaded afterlength, four pickup settings and an active EQ/wah measured against the 2014 factory performance. [Zacharias, US 3,447,410](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/03/ff/9d/616c0f2fd28202/US3447410.pdf) · [Bilbao and Rath, time-domain Clavinet model](https://aes.org/publications/elibrary/elibrary-page/?id=15311) · [Gabrielli et al., D6 analysis, §§2–3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1687-6180-2013-103)

## D6 — first build

The [three-key offline prototype](../../clavinet-d6/README.md) supplies separate pickups, a loaded output jack and measured attack, switch and key-up comparisons for B♭2, E4 and F♯5. Its reference session includes a scrub-able string-action diagram driven by the simulated grid. The [BC550C board model](../../clavinet-d6/research/electronics/README.md) covers the four pickup positions and sixteen tone-tab states. Electric Pianos now exposes the D6 native F1–E6 action, both solos and both paired positions. Register geometry beyond the prototype and absolute pickup sensitivity remain estimated.

**Physical target.** One string per key, F1–E6 (MIDI 29–88). The investigated D6 had 23 wound strings followed by 37 plain strings. The key drives a rubber tangent into the string and clamps it against a metal anvil for the duration of the note. The tailpiece bounds the speaking length. On key-up, the length toward the tuning pin rejoins the vibrating string and the woven yarn absorbs its energy. A second damper, the player-operated mute bar, can touch the speaking strings while the keys are down. [Hohner D6 manual, “How the Clavinet Works” and “Tone Colours”](https://www.clavinet.com/clavinet_d6.pdf) · [D6 measurement and action diagram, fig. 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1687-6180-2013-103)

1. **Build a three-key mechanical reference.** Start with one wound bass, one middle plain string and one treble. Enter speaking and afterlength in metres, core and winding mass per unit length, measured pitch, tangent travel, anvil position and yarn contact span. Where tension is inaccessible, infer a starting value from length, mass and pitch and flag it as derived. Implement a damped stiff string and two unilateral contacts, one against the compliant driven tip and one against the rigid stop. During travel, contact force excites the string. At full dip the clamp defines the speaking boundary. On release, preserve the displacement and velocity of both segments as the boundary opens. Derive MIDI-to-key speed from repeated soft and hard strikes. Log displacement at the anvil and both pickups plus contact work, loss and stored energy. A finite-difference reference can run offline at a finer step until further refinement leaves those observations stable. [Inventor’s action, fig. 1](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/03/ff/9d/616c0f2fd28202/US3447410.pdf) · [Bilbao and Rath](https://aes.org/publications/elibrary/elibrary-page/?id=15311)
2. **Separate the two pickup voltages.** Read transverse string motion at each pickup’s distance from the tailpiece, including the slant of the bridge pickup above the strings and the central pickup below them. Integrate flux over each pickup’s effective width and derive voltage from its time derivative. Carry winding polarity once, with an explicit positive string-motion convention. Check geometry against spectral nulls, then fit gap sensitivity and electrical impedance. The DAFx paper supplies inspection and impedance data. Its companion audio illustrates *synthesized* gap changes. [Remaggi et al., pickup measurements](https://www.dafx12.york.ac.uk/papers/dafx12_submission_62.pdf) · [Authors’ gap comparisons](https://research.spa.aalto.fi/publications/papers/dafx12-pickup/)
3. **Reconstruct the specific preamp.** Trace D6 drawing 710314 from the pickups through switching and the input transformer to the transistor stages, four switched tone branches, volume and loaded output. Retain coil/transformer loading in parallel pickup states. In the Chicago shop’s documented configuration, A+C selects the central pickup, B+C the bridge, A+D both in phase and B+D both opposed. Measure polarity at the terminals on the reference unit. Repair technicians report later D6 instruments with E7-style switching and component substitutions. Record board photographs, serial and fitted values. Exercise all 4 × 16 pickup/tone states, including silence when all four tone tabs are off. [Hohner D6 schematic](https://www.clavinet.com/Clavinet_D6_schematic.pdf) · [Service shop’s switch map](https://chicagoelectricpiano.com/blogs/news/clavinet-controls-and-settings) · [Shop’s observed wiring revisions](https://chicagoelectricpiano.com/blogs/news/clavinet-schematic-wiring)
4. **Extend and reduce for live use.** Add keys on both sides of the wound/plain break and verify each register before interpolation across remaining notes. If the full contact solver is too expensive, compare a dispersive waveguide with a dynamic tangent junction and distributed loss against the reference at both pickup taps. Preserve motion on repeated strikes. Fit a frame mode driven by anvil force only if the reference DI reveals the knock after the string and pickup contributions are accounted for. The published fitted excitation pulse and recorded knock provide comparison cases for the physical solver. [D6 tone analysis, §§2.3 and 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1687-6180-2013-103) · [Recorded D6 singles and riff, alongside separately identified synthesis examples](https://research.spa.aalto.fi/publications/papers/icmc11-clavinet/)

**First acceptance session.** Record dry output, two solo pickups, mute endpoints, soft/medium/hard strikes, held notes, short releases and same-key restrikes. Include MIDI 51 and 52 at the measured D6 string break. Log key velocity or travel, level calibration, circuit revision, instrument tuning, pickup gap and DI load. Fit string pitch, partial spacing and decay on held notes first. Then compare the attack pulse, pitch change at release, time to lose 20 dB after release, pickup-specific nulls and the muted spectrum. Reserve a key per register, a dynamic and two-pickup chords for validation. Use repeated captures to set the uncertainty for each metric and publish residuals per condition. For electronics, compare the implementation to a circuit simulation and loaded bench sweep, then measure gain compression on loud chords. Acceptance requires reproducing the held-out behavior and the complete switch truth table. The public hardware examples can start the work while an identified unit is being sourced.

## E7 — second circuit and regulation

The [playable E7 and measurement report](../../clavinet-d6/research/e7/README.md) document circuit 801 111, the regulated supply, the fixed-input comparison and three notes from an E7 owner’s chromatic run. All 64 jack transfers agree with independent ngspice results within 0.0007 dB and 0.0024°. The model borrows the D6 string geometry. The largest recorded spectral residual is on F♯5, and E7-specific release and mute measurements remain open.

Hohner’s E7 manual gives drawing **801 111**, the transformer specification, supply circuitry, separate power switch and sliding volume control. Use the D6 string/contact implementation with a fresh E7 measurement sheet. Enter its string lengths, pickup positions/gaps and mute engagement before judging audible differences. Transcribe the E7 supply and two audio stages, transformer wiring and loaded tone branches. Trace each switch in both positions against the dedicated circuit drawing. Build the oscillator-free circuit test first by injecting known signals into the two pickup inputs. [Hohner E7 manual, controls and schematic on pp. 5–6](https://www.clavinet.com/clavinet_e7.pdf) · [E7 circuit drawings](https://www.clavinet.com/Clavinet_E7_schematic.pdf)

**Decision gate.** Compare D6 and E7 circuits under the same calibrated injection. Then compare each to its own reference instrument with solos, combined pickups, each tone branch, full-level chords and mute on/off. Record gain, headroom and response differences attributable to circuit values separately from differences caused by setup. An E7 netlist and matching transfer curves establish the electronics milestone. A dated E7 dry capture across registers and release conditions is still needed for instrument voicing.

## Clavinet/Pianet Duo — combine two actions under one key

The Duo engine now drives a tangent and suction pad from the same key. Its pickup banks split between C3 (MIDI 48, TA20) and C♯3 (49, TA21). Changing the selector during a hold can reveal either action already ringing. The bench records the two slider outputs and the mixed jack on one clock. Comparison with the transistor netlist covers 99 control combinations, both electrical inputs and all three outputs. A simultaneous hardware capture is still needed to measure the relative timing of the two actions and their output balance. [Engine](../../hohner-duo/src/engine.rs) · [Circuit verification](../../hohner-duo/research/circuit.py) · [Hohner Duo drawing](https://www.clavinet.com/Clavinet_Pianet_Duo_Schematic.pdf)

The factory manual documents a string tangent and a pad-plucked magnetized reed with a series-connected pickup system. One key moves both actions. Hohner describes much greater touch sensitivity on the Clavinet branch. The mute slider and four tone tabs affect the strings. The instrument has two level sliders, a footswitch for changing registrations, separate and combined outputs and a recording output that separates the instruments. [Hohner Duo manual, printed pp. 11–15](https://anaphonic.com/wp-content/uploads/hohner_pianetclavinetduo_sm.pdf) · [Duo schematic](https://www.clavinet.com/Clavinet_Pianet_Duo_Schematic.pdf)

1. Once the two actions pass independently, drive both from the same key travel. Calibrate the relative time of reed detachment and string impact using simultaneous branch recordings. Measure the Duo’s reed and pickup-bank wiring against the standalone T plan. Retain separate mechanical states for every held key so changing registration can reveal an already-ringing branch. [Magnetic Pianet plan](plucked-reeds.md)
2. Trace the Duo’s pickup banks, selector contacts, two level controls and output paths from its own schematic. Transcribe the manual’s preset-tab and footswitch truth table and its three fixed split registrations (Pianet high/Clavinet low, reverse, and both high/Pianet low). Confirm the precise C/C♯ boundary by testing adjacent keys on the unit and recording the MIDI numbers. Check for switching or bleed at the bank outputs when changing registration mid-note.
3. Capture both isolated outputs plus combined output at matched gain. Verify the independent velocity curves, onset offset, both sides of the split, each footswitch state and Clavinet-only mute. The layer should sum with the balance, phase and loading of the real combined jack. Validate routing before judging the blended timbre. The standalone T and D6 recordings can develop the two mechanisms. A paired Duo capture establishes their shared-key regulation.

## Vibanet — factory performance and remaining bench measurements

Vintage Vibe documents custom pickups and strings, factory Clavigel damping, three-band fixed EQ and onboard auto-wah. The two magnetic pickups and mechanical mute are also described by the developer of the official virtual instrument. The builder offers a related retrofit preamp. The factory instrument, its fitted board revision and its dry output are the reference here. A component schematic for that board was not established in this research. [Vintage Vibe’s instrument description and action photographs](https://www.vintagevibe.com/blogs/news/get-funky-with-the-vibanet) · [Official virtual instrument partner’s hardware description](https://www.spectrasonics.net/products/keyscape/models/vintage-vibe-vibanet/) · [Builder’s retrofit kit](https://www.vintagevibe.com/products/clavinet-vibanet-preamp-kit)

The [playable Vibanet and recording comparison](../../clavinet-d6/research/vibanet/README.md) cover the four pickup settings, the fifteen labelled EQ positions, nine dry releases and four wah strikes. Held-out F2 release times are 88–94 ms on the recording and 114 ms in the model. B♭2 releases too slowly and the F♯2 wah sweep remains a poor match. An isolated bench recording could resolve these differences.

1. Measure lengths, gauges, both pickup locations, polarities and height by register. Fit gel contact loss from repeated key-up traces, then use mute endpoints and graduated strikes to separate the gel, mute bar and tangent effects. Reuse the D6 contact equations with these measured inputs.
2. Photograph the actual panel and board, enumerate pickup/EQ combinations, and identify where the envelope detector taps the signal. Measure bypassed response, dry gain, clipping, and EQ responses with known input and load. With auto-wah engaged, use level steps, bursts and two-tone chords at several sensitivities to measure sweep limits, resonance, detector attack and recovery. Exercise it after summing strings if the tap is on the instrument bus. Capture an isolated second note during detector recovery to test that placement.
3. Validate dry before wet against reserved notes and repeated chords. A documented board trace supports component-level work. Without one, a measured transfer and envelope model may be reported as a bench-characterized approximation with its tested signal range. The current Vibanet reconstructs the filmed performance. A specimen-level electrical reconstruction remains open.
