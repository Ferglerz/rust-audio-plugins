# Shared DSP, calibration and plugin integration

The **Electric Pianos** CLAP/VST3 wrapper hosts the 28 selections in the family roadmap. Its original three-position piano parameter retains its saved automation thresholds. Separate model selectors recall the additional instruments and circuits. Give each new mechanism an offline note probe first. The smallest useful common contract is a timestamped key or pedal action, a persistent physical key, and an observable output at a named terminal. A Pianet N reed at release, a D6 string at the anvil and a CP hammer in flight all need different internal states. ## What to share, and when

The shipped Vintage Vibe, MK8 and Baldwin use the same MIDI and pointer key ownership, choke ramps, and per-note output iteration. Each supplies its own hammer and vibrating tine or string. The two Vintage Vibe boards share the same harp. The D6 and Vibanet share a tangent-key bank with independent post-pickup circuits. Yamaha’s original 73-key pickup network and the Baldwin share a loaded piezo element solver, with their respective element counts, capacitances and loads. During extraction the D6’s 37 reference renders, the Duo’s 92 renders and the passive Vintage Vibe’s 192 probe renders retained identical samples. ## Signals and ownership

1. **Action → resonator.** Specify the strike or pluck point in metres, velocity in m/s, and either force in N integrated over time or a displacement constraint. Apply the contact force to all coupled modes at its physical point. Preserve both displacement and velocity when the pad detaches, tangent releases, key restrikes or damper engages. For prototypes, export contact force, release time and mechanical energy alongside audio.
2. **Resonator → pickup.** Read the required physical quantity at its location. For magnetic sensing use induced EMF from changing flux linkage. For a biased electrode solve the changing charge through the actual bias and input load. For a piezo use force or strain at the bearing/rail to drive charge into capacitance. The RF Cembalet requires its capacitance-dependent tuned circuit and detector. Give quantities units and polarities at the point they cross modules.
3. **Pickups → output.** Assemble the actual series, parallel, grouped or switched source network before the panel. Electrically connected silent keys still present impedance. Apply a common detector, saturating preamp, swell or tremolo at its documented position. Make available the mechanical readout, pickup bus, pre-effects/patch and loaded jack wherever the hardware offers corresponding observations.
4. **Host → engine.** One physical key keeps its identity through release and restrike. The wrapper owns event timing, keyboard feedback, choke and transport/reset handling. Each instrument owns its physical compass, the meaning of key release and the pedals it actually has. Expose the hardware range by default. Keep any extended notes identifiable as extrapolations and keep a real note-off addressable after a range switch.

## Evidence needed to advance a model

| Milestone | Artifact and acceptance |
| --- | --- |
| Establish the reference instrument | Record maker, model, serial/board revision, original or replacement contact parts, regulation, source page/figure and actual signal tap. Give each fitted parameter a unit and a status of documented, measured, fitted or assumed. For a patent, record which features a production photo, drawing or specimen confirms. |
| One mechanism works | Render a low, middle and high key, including a material or wiring boundary. Capture repeated soft, medium and hard actions, key-up, restrike and physical pedal states. For a pluck, include different downstroke and reseating speeds. Separate mechanical spectra from pickup-generated harmonics using motion or an intermediate tap. Keep a per-condition uncertainty estimate from repeats. |
| Circuit works independently | Rebuild the stock wiring in an offline deck or circuit solver and apply the service-manual source, load and control settings. Compare the live implementation to that reference with complex sweeps, switch truth tables, drive levels and modulation phase. A proposed first target for a linearized passband is 0.25 dB magnitude error against the deck. Use the hardware document's own tolerances for factory tests. Record outliers and the region near a deep null separately. |
| It sounds like an identified unit | Reserve entire keys, a playing strength and a short phrase before fitting. Compare pitch in cents, partial levels in dB, beating in Hz, decay in dB/s, contact/damper times in ms and relative level across dynamics. Use repeatability and measurement resolution to set per-observable acceptance bands before fitting. Publish worst keys as well as aggregate results and fixed-gain blind A/Bs. Label unknown gain, velocity, editing, tuning or microphone response. Keep audio clips in research and use coefficients in the runtime. |
| Real-time reduction works | Check the same events against a converged higher-rate reference at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, especially hard treble and dense chords driving a nonlinear bus. Test silence, pedal catch, repeated notes and maximum sustain. Record worst and percentile callback times at 64 frames on a named machine. The block budgets are about 1.45, 1.33 and 0.67 ms respectively, and the host needs time too. Record startup cost and memory with all installed models selectable. [CP-80 measurement precedent and stress-case limits](../../cp80/README.md) |

When a comparison fails, use the earliest observable that disagrees. Wrong mechanical partial frequencies point to geometry, boundary or stiffness. Correct motion but wrong polarity or spectral nulls points to the pickup. Correct low-level sweeps but incorrect loud chords point to the shared output stage or supply. A DI recording alone rarely identifies hammer stiffness, pickup sensitivity and amplifier gain independently.

## Integrate a proven model

The installed 28-model CLAP on the arm64 development Mac took 0.405 s to create and initialize in a fresh minimal host. Two 128-frame activations each at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz took 0.388–0.534 s. The host’s maximum resident set over that sequence was 36.2 MiB, including all selectable engines. These wall times include contention from other builds. Sample-rate changes rebuild the banks during host activation. During playback all engines have prepared key storage, and the wrapper renders only the selected instrument. [Host initialization and rendering](../../openwurli-ui/src/lib.rs)

With the MK8 selected, repeated host transport resets on the installed CLAP took 0.04–0.05 ms at all three rates after the first reset had settled the inactive Wurli circuit. Before reusing that settled state they took 72–78 ms at 48 kHz. A host reset with Wurli selected still runs its original analogue startup sequence, and returning from two inactive resets reproduced the former Wurli output sample for sample after prior note and controller activity. [Reset and model selection](../../openwurli-ui/src/lib.rs)

The final installed CLAP produced a finite, nonzero signal at every MIDI pitch from 0 through 127 on all 28 selections at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz. Each adjacent pair of pitches produced distinct samples. The five adhesive or suction pad actions also passed stock, sustained and pedal-release comparisons at all three rates. The interactive cutaways follow the caught pad independently of the released key.
